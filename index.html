Basic Principles

Five habits that show up in every recipe in this book

1. Pat it dry

Water is the enemy of browning. A wet surface has to boil off its water at 212°F before it can brown, so it steams instead of sears.

  • Paper towels on every piece of meat, fish, or shrimp before it hits the pan. Press, wait a few minutes, press again.
  • Vegetables too: wet green beans and Brussels sprouts steam instead of blister.
  • For crispy chicken skin, air-dry uncovered in the fridge overnight on a rack. The skin dries out like parchment.

The tell: if it sizzles hard the second it hits the pan, it was dry enough. If it hisses and spits, it wasn't.

2. Dry brine for flavor

Salt the meat ahead of time and leave it uncovered in the fridge. Salt pulls moisture out, dissolves into it, and gets pulled back into the meat — seasoning it all the way through and helping it hold onto juice while it cooks.

Rule Amount
The 1% rule 1 tsp Morton kosher salt per pound of meat
Steaks, chops, chicken pieces 1 hour minimum, overnight best
Big roasts, pork butt, ribs 12–24 hours
Shrimp, scallops, fish Last minute only (10–15 min max) — longer turns them mushy
Ground meat Salt the surface right before cooking, never mixed in early
Wet brine (when you want one) 2% salt by weight of the water

Set it on a rack over a sheet pan so air reaches every side. All salt amounts in this book are for Morton kosher salt. Diamond Crystal is lighter — use almost double.

3. Use cast iron to sear

Cast iron holds heat. When cold meat hits it, the pan doesn't drop in temperature, so you get a crust instead of gray meat.

  • Preheat longer than you think. 5 minutes on medium-high to high. A drop of water should skitter and vanish instantly.
  • Thin film of high-smoke-point oil (avocado, canola, peanut). Butter goes in later, after the flip.
  • Don't crowd. Space between pieces, or the steam can't escape. Two batches beat one soggy batch.
  • Don't touch it. Food sticks until it's browned, then lets go on its own. If it won't release, it's not ready.
  • Care: Rinse hot, scrub with salt if needed, dry on the burner, wipe with a few drops of oil. Enameled Dutch ovens are different — medium-high max, wood or silicone tools.

4. Taste as you cook

The recipe gets you close. Your tongue finishes the job.

  • Taste at every stage, not just at the end. Fry a test bite of raw mixtures like burger meat.
  • When it tastes flat, ask two questions in order: Does it need salt? Does it need acid? Most of the time it's one of the two.
  • Heavy or muddy? Acid — a splash of vinegar or squeeze of lemon. Not more salt.
  • Too salty? Acid, fat, or more of the unsalted base. You can't take salt out.
  • Cold food needs more seasoning. Taste chilled dishes cold, then adjust.
  • Adjust a little at a time. You can always add more.

5. Cook to temperature, not time

Every cut, pan, and grill is different. A thermometer is the single best tool in the kitchen.

  • Use a probe thermometer like the CHEF iQ (wireless, leave it in, the phone alerts you), plus a fast instant-read for spot checks.
  • Insert into the thickest part, from the side, away from bone.
  • Pull early. Food keeps climbing 3–10°F while it rests (carryover). Bigger cuts climb more.
Food Pull at Notes
Scallops 115°F Climb to ~120–125°F resting
Salmon 125°F Medium
Steak, medium-rare 125–130°F Rest 5–10 min
Cod and white fish 140°F Finishes ~145°F
Pork chops / tenderloin 140°F Finishes ~145°F
Chicken breast 160°F Finishes 165°F
Ground beef, sausage burgers 160°F Safe for pork and egg
Chicken thighs and wings 175–190°F Safe at 165°F, better higher
Pork butt (to shred) 190°F, then hold Carryover in the cooler finishes it
Ribs 195°F, then glaze Bend test beats the thermometer
Beef chuck, country ribs (braised) 195–200°F Fork-tender is the real test

The Five Pillars — Quick Reference

Pillar What it does Rules of thumb
Salt Turns up flavor, turns down bitterness 1% by weight. Salt early for big cuts, late for seafood
Fat Carries flavor, makes heat work High-smoke-point oil to sear, butter and good oil to finish
Acid Brightens, cuts richness Acid twice: some in the cook, a splash at the end. 3 oil : 1 vinegar for vinaigrette
Sweet Balances acid and heat, builds browning Sugar off high heat. Sauces and glazes go on last
Umami Savory depth Stack it: soy, fish sauce, anchovy, mushrooms, tomato paste, Parmesan, a hard sear

Peanut Butter Steel-Cut Overnight Oats

No-cook fridge batch for the week, finished with berries, flake salt & honey

Yield7 servings (~1 cup each)
Active10 min
Soakovernight, then all week
Keeps6 days refrigerated
EquipmentLarge lidded container (3 qt) or 7 jars, whisk

Ingredients

The batch

  • 5 cups unsweetened almond milk, divided
  • ¼ cup peanut butter powder
  • ½ tsp Morton kosher salt
  • 2 cups steel-cut oats
  • ¼ cup chia seeds

To serve (per bowl)

  • ½ cup berries (fresh or thawed frozen)
  • Pinch of flake salt (Maldon or similar)
  • 1–2 tsp honey

Steps

  1. Dissolve the PB powder. Whisk peanut butter powder and salt into 1 cup of almond milk until smooth. Added dry, it clumps.
  2. Combine. In the container, stir together oats, chia, the PB mixture, and the remaining 4 cups almond milk.
  3. Stir again in 10 minutes. Chia clumps as it starts to gel. A second stir breaks it up.
  4. Refrigerate. Lid on. Overnight minimum.
  5. Eat all week. The oats keep softening in the fridge as you go. Stir the batch before scooping each day.
  6. Top each bowl. Berries → honey drizzle → pinch of flake salt.

What to expect through the week

  • Day 1: Chewy, nutty, al dente.
  • Days 2–4: The sweet spot — tender with a little bite.
  • Days 5–6: Softest and thickest. Loosen with a splash of almond milk.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Salt dissolved in the milk seasons the oats as they soak. Flake salt on top hits your tongue first — it makes honey taste sweeter and peanut butter taste nuttier.

Fat. Almond milk and peanut butter powder keep it light. PB powder has most of the fat pressed out, so you get peanut flavor and protein without heaviness. Chia adds omega-3 fats and creaminess.

Acid. Berries bring brightness. Without them, the bowl tastes flat and heavy.

Sweet. No sugar in the batch. Honey goes on top, per bowl — sweetness on the surface reads stronger than sweetness mixed in, so you use less.

Umami. Toasted peanut and the nutty grain flavor of steel-cut oats give savory depth.

Time instead of heat. Steel-cut oats are chopped whole groats, not rolled flat. Cold soaking softens them slowly over days instead of minutes, which keeps a chewy texture rolled oats can't hold. Chia gels around them so the batch stays creamy, not watery.

Variants

  • Hot: Splash of almond milk, microwave 90 seconds, stir, 30 more seconds.
  • Softer from day 1: Toast the dry oats in a skillet 3–4 minutes until nutty, cool, then soak. Or pour 1 cup of the almond milk in warm.
  • Banana: Mash 1 ripe banana into the batch; cut honey in half.
  • More protein: Whisk a scoop of vanilla protein powder into the PB mixture. Add ½ cup more almond milk.
  • Toppings: Toasted pecans, cacao nibs, sliced banana, or a spoon of real peanut butter.

Nutritious Enhancements

Add How much Why
Ground flaxseed 3 Tbsp stirred into the batch + ¼ cup extra almond milk Omega-3 fats and fiber. Must be ground — whole seeds pass through undigested
Plain Greek yogurt ¼ cup swirled into each bowl Protein and probiotics. Its tang adds the acid this bowl needs
Hemp hearts 1 Tbsp sprinkled per bowl Complete plant protein (~3 g per Tbsp) and magnesium. Mild, nutty
Toasted walnuts 1 Tbsp chopped per bowl Omega-3s and crunch. Toast 5 min in a dry pan; add at serving so they stay crisp
Cacao nibs 1 tsp per bowl Fiber, magnesium, and antioxidants with no sugar. The bitterness balances the honey

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Too thick late in the week Normal — chia and oats keep absorbing. Stir in a splash of almond milk
Too chewy Give it another day, or try the toasted/warm-milk variant
PB powder clumps Whisk into milk first, never straight into the batch
Chia in clumps Stir again 10 minutes after mixing
Bland Needs salt. Don't skip the flake salt finish

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Sweet food needs salt too. Salt doesn't just make food salty — it turns down bitterness and turns up sweetness. That's why a pinch of flake salt makes honey taste like more honey.

Notes for the Kids

  • Salt in oatmeal sounds weird. Do it anyway — it's the difference between good and great.
  • Ten minutes on Sunday night and breakfast is done for the week.
  • It gets better every day. Day 3 is the best bowl.
  • Honey and flake salt go on last, on top, where you'll taste them.

Loaded Packet Ramen

Instant ramen built up with seared mushrooms, steamed broccoli, poached eggs & chili oil

Serves2
Active20 min
Total20 min
EquipmentLarge pot with steamer basket, skillet, small saucepan (for eggs), ladle, 2 large bowls, slotted spoon

Order of Operations

  1. Big pot of water on to boil. Small saucepan of water on to simmer.
  2. While water heats: slice mushrooms and green onions, cut broccoli, toast sesame seeds.
  3. Mushrooms into the skillet.
  4. Broccoli steams over the big pot.
  5. Eggs into the small pan.
  6. Noodles into the big pot. Premix the packets in the bowls.
  7. Build bowls.

Ingredients

Base

  • 2 packets instant ramen, your choice (noodles + seasoning + any oil packets)

Mushrooms

  • 8 oz mushrooms, sliced ¼ inch — shiitake, cremini, or a mix
  • 1 Tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 tsp soy sauce
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • Pinch of salt

Broccoli

  • 2 cups broccoli florets, bite-size

Eggs

  • 4 large eggs, cold (2 per bowl)
  • 1 Tbsp rice vinegar or white vinegar

Finish

  • 2 green onions, thin-sliced on a bias
  • 1 tsp white sesame seeds
  • 1 tsp black sesame seeds
  • Chili oil (chili crisp works too), to taste
  • 1 tsp rice vinegar or 1 lime wedge per bowl

Steps

1. Set up the water

  • Big pot: 3 quarts water, rolling boil. This cooks noodles, steams broccoli, and supplies the broth water.
  • Small saucepan: 3 inches of water + vinegar, bring to a bare simmer — small bubbles on the bottom, surface barely moving.

2. Toast sesame seeds

Dry skillet, medium heat, 2–3 minutes, shaking, until the white seeds turn golden and smell nutty. Tip into a small bowl. Wipe the skillet.

3. Pan-fry the mushrooms

  1. Skillet over medium-high until hot. Add oil.
  2. Mushrooms in a single layer. Don't stir for 3 minutes — let them brown.
  3. Stir, cook 2–3 minutes more until golden all over.
  4. Add garlic for the last 30 seconds. Splash in soy, toss, take off heat. Salt to taste.

4. Steam the broccoli

Steamer basket over the boiling big pot, lid on, 3–4 minutes until bright green and just tender. Set aside.

5. Poach the eggs

  1. Crack each egg into a small cup.
  2. Swirl the simmering water gently. Slide eggs in one at a time.
  3. Cook 3½–4 minutes for set whites and runny yolks.
  4. Lift out with a slotted spoon. Rest on a paper towel.

6. Noodles and broth

  1. Premix the packet: In each serving bowl, add the seasoning packet (and oil packet, if any). Ladle in about ½ cup boiling water from the big pot. Whisk into a smooth paste-broth. This dissolves the seasoning evenly — no salty clumps at the bottom.
  2. Cook noodles: Into the big pot, 1 minute less than the package says. They finish softening in the hot broth.
  3. Top off the broth: Add more boiling pot water to each bowl to the amount the packet calls for (usually 1½–2 cups total). Taste.
  4. Noodles in: Tongs the noodles into the bowls.

7. Build the bowl

Noodles → broccoli and mushrooms on one side → 2 poached eggs on top → green onions → both sesame seeds → chili oil to taste → ½–1 tsp rice vinegar or a lime squeeze over the broth. Taste and add more acid if it still feels heavy.

Break the yolk into the broth as you eat.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. The packet does most of the work. Premixing in a small amount of water first dissolves it completely so the salt is even through the whole bowl. Soy on the mushrooms seasons them to match the broth.

Fat. The runny yolk enriches the broth as it mixes in. Chili oil and toasted sesame seeds add aromatic fat on top, where you smell it first.

Acid. Packet ramen has almost none, which is why it can taste heavy. A small splash of rice vinegar or lime at the very end brightens the broth and makes the salt and umami taste cleaner — acid late, as always.

Sweet. Seared mushrooms and steamed broccoli bring natural sweetness from browning and fresh vegetables. Green onion adds a sharp-sweet bite.

Umami. The big one. Ramen seasoning is loaded with glutamate. Mushrooms — especially shiitake — carry guanylate, a nucleotide that multiplies glutamate's savory effect when they meet. That synergy is why mushrooms make packet ramen taste homemade.

Heat (temperature). High-heat searing browns the mushrooms (Maillard flavor) instead of steaming them gray. Gentle simmering keeps poached eggs tender. Undercooking the noodles by a minute keeps them springy in the hot broth.

Variants

  • Jammy eggs: Boil 6½ minutes, ice bath, peel. Make several ahead for the week.
  • Protein: Leftover pulled pork from the Smoked Pork Butt, rotisserie chicken, or seared tofu.
  • Greens: Bok choy or spinach — drop into the noodle pot for the last 30 seconds.
  • Richer broth: Stir 1 tsp miso or ½ tsp sesame oil into the premix.
  • Crunch: Bean sprouts, sliced radish, or a sheet of nori.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Mushrooms soggy and gray Pan wasn't hot, crowded, or stirred too soon. Cook in two batches
Poached eggs wispy Water boiling too hard, or eggs not fresh. Keep it at a bare simmer; strain eggs through a fine mesh to drop the loose white
Broth too salty Add more pot water; use ¾ of the seasoning packet
Broth bland Splash of soy or a pinch more packet seasoning
Noodles mushy Pull them a minute early; serve right away
Sesame seeds burned Black seeds don't change color — watch the white ones

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • When salt isn't the answer, try acid. A dish that tastes heavy or muddy often needs brightness, not more salt. A small splash of vinegar at the end is the fix.
  • Season the water that cooks your food. Food takes on the flavor of what it cooks in — which is why the packet goes into the broth, and the noodles finish in it.

Notes for the Kids

  • Don't dump the packet into the pot. Mix it in the bowl first.
  • One pot of boiling water does three jobs. Work smart.
  • Leave the mushrooms alone in the pan. Browning takes patience.
  • Fresh eggs poach better. Old eggs spread out.
  • Any packet works. Find your favorite and make it better.

Ground Beef Bulgogi Rice Bowls

Sweet-savory crusted beef over rice with quick cucumbers, kimchi & a fried egg

Serves4
Active30 min
Total35 min
Equipment12-inch cast iron or heavy skillet, rice pot or pressure cooker, small bowls for sauce and pickles, box grater or microplane

Order of Operations

  1. Rice on.
  2. Quick cucumbers (they need 15 minutes).
  3. Bulgogi sauce and gochujang drizzle.
  4. Beef.
  5. Optional garnishes: toast almonds before the beef; fry eggs in the same skillet after.
  6. Build bowls.

Ingredients

Rice

  • 1½ cups short-grain or jasmine rice
  • 1½ cups water

Quick-pickled cucumbers

  • 1 English cucumber, thin-sliced
  • 2 Tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • Pinch of gochugaru or red pepper flakes

Bulgogi sauce

  • ¼ cup soy sauce
  • 3 Tbsp brown sugar, packed
  • ¼ cup grated Asian pear or apple (~½ small)
  • 4 garlic cloves, grated
  • 1 Tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • ¼ tsp black pepper

Beef

  • 1 lb ground beef, 85/15
  • 1 tsp neutral oil
  • 1 small yellow onion, thin-sliced
  • 1 Tbsp toasted sesame oil
  • 1 Tbsp rice vinegar

Gochujang drizzle

  • 2 Tbsp gochujang
  • 1 Tbsp honey
  • 1 Tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • 1–2 Tbsp water to thin

To finish

  • 1 cup kimchi, chopped
  • 2 carrots, julienned or shredded
  • 3 green onions, thin-sliced
  • 1 tsp each white and black sesame seeds, toasted

Optional garnishes

  • 4 large eggs, fried
  • ¼ cup sliced almonds, toasted
  • 2 Tbsp crispy fried shallots (store-bought)
  • 1 sheet roasted nori, cut into thin strips
  • Handful of cilantro leaves

Steps

1. Rice

Rinse rice in a strainer until the water runs mostly clear. Combine with water.

  • Stovetop: Boil, then cover, lowest heat 15 minutes. Off heat, rest covered 10 minutes.
  • Pressure cooker: High pressure 4 minutes (jasmine) or 6 (short-grain), natural release 10 minutes.

2. Quick cucumbers

Toss cucumbers with vinegar, sugar, salt, and gochugaru. Set aside at least 15 minutes, tossing once.

3. Sauces

  • Bulgogi sauce: Whisk everything until the sugar dissolves.
  • Gochujang drizzle: Whisk until smooth and pourable. Squeeze bottle if you have one.

4. The beef

  1. Heat hard. Skillet over medium-high until very hot. Add oil.
  2. Press and sear. Add beef in one big patty, press flat across the pan, and leave it alone for 4 minutes until a deep brown crust forms.
  3. Break and flip. Break into large chunks, flip, sear 2 minutes more.
  4. Onions. Push beef aside, add onions to the hot spot, cook 2–3 minutes until softened and browned at the edges. Break beef into bite-size crumbles.
  5. Drain. Tilt the pan and spoon off all but about 1 Tbsp fat.
  6. Sauce last. Lower heat to medium. Pour in the bulgogi sauce. Simmer, stirring, 2–3 minutes until it reduces to a glossy glaze that clings to the beef.
  7. Finish off heat. Stir in sesame oil and rice vinegar. Taste. Scrape into a bowl and cover.

5. Optional garnishes

  • Almonds: Dry skillet, medium heat, 3–4 minutes, stirring, until golden and fragrant. Do this before the beef.
  • Fried eggs: Wipe the skillet, a little oil, medium-high. Crack in eggs, cook until whites are set and edges are crisp and lacy, yolks runny, about 2–3 minutes. Season with a pinch of salt.

6. Build the bowls

Rice → bulgogi beef → cucumbers, kimchi, and carrots in sections around the edge → green onions → sesame seeds → gochujang drizzle. Set out the optional garnishes so everyone adds their own.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Soy sauce is the salt. The rice stays plain on purpose — it's the neutral base that balances the intensely seasoned beef. Kimchi and pickled cucumbers add more salt in small, sharp bites.

Fat. 85/15 beef has enough fat to sear well and stay juicy; draining the excess keeps the glaze from going greasy. Sesame oil goes in at the end, off heat, because its flavor fades with heat. An optional runny yolk becomes a sauce for the rice.

Acid. Rice vinegar at three points — beef finish, cucumbers, drizzle — plus fermented kimchi. Acid cuts the sweet glaze and keeps the bowl from tasting heavy.

Sweet. Brown sugar and grated pear are classic bulgogi. Sauce goes in after searing at lower heat, so the sugar glazes instead of burning — the book's "sugar off high heat" rule.

Umami. A big crust on the beef (Maillard browning) + soy + kimchi + gochujang drizzle (fermented chili paste) stack layers of savory, fermented depth. Nori adds a sea-savory hit.

Heat (temperature + spice). Pressing the beef flat and leaving it alone builds a crust ground beef usually never gets. The beef stays mild; all the chili heat lives in the drizzle so each person controls it.

Variants

  • Leaner: 90/10 beef or ground turkey; skip draining.
  • Lettuce wraps: Spoon beef and toppings into butter lettuce cups; skip rice.
  • Veggies: Add sautéed spinach with garlic and sesame oil, or bean sprouts.
  • Kid-friendly: Already mild — serve the drizzle on the side.
  • Meal prep: Beef, rice, and cucumbers keep 4 days. Fry eggs fresh; keep almonds and shallots separate so they stay crisp.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Beef gray, no crust Pan not hot enough or stirred too soon. Press it flat and wait
Glaze burned Heat too high when sauce went in. Lower to medium first
Too sweet Add another splash of rice vinegar or soy
Too salty Use low-sodium soy next time; add more rice and cucumbers to the bowl
Greasy Drain more fat before adding sauce
Rice gummy Rinse more thoroughly; don't lift the lid while it cooks

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Sugar burns. Sugar browns faster than meat. Build the crust first, then add sweet sauces at lower heat.
  • Fats have flavors. Neutral oil for high heat; toasted sesame oil off the heat, because its aroma fades when cooked hard.

Notes for the Kids

  • Don't stir the beef. Press it flat and let it brown. That crust is the whole point.
  • Sauce goes in last. Sugar burns fast on high heat.
  • Every bite should have a little of everything — sweet beef, sour pickle, spicy drizzle, rich egg.
  • Grate the pear. It's the secret most people skip.

Veggie Rice Bowls

Seared mushrooms & spinach, hot-pan vegetables, a runny egg, and three sauces

Serves4
Active35 min
Total40 min
Equipment12-inch cast iron or wok, rice pot or pressure cooker, 2 small jars or bowls for sauces

Order of Operations

  1. Rice on.
  2. Make the sauces.
  3. Prep all vegetables — cut everything before the pan gets hot.
  4. Mushrooms and spinach. Set aside.
  5. Wipe the pan. Hot-pan veggie mix.
  6. Fry eggs in the same pan.
  7. Build bowls.

Ingredients

Rice

  • 1½ cups short-grain or jasmine rice + 1½ cups water
  • Method: see Ground Beef Bulgogi Rice Bowls, Step 1

Sauce A — Peanut-Lime (creamy, nutty, sweet-tangy)

  • ¼ cup creamy peanut butter
  • 2 Tbsp soy sauce
  • 2 Tbsp lime juice (~1 lime)
  • 1 Tbsp honey
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • 1 garlic clove, grated
  • ½ tsp grated ginger
  • 1 tsp sriracha (optional)
  • 3–4 Tbsp warm water to thin

Sauce B — Ginger-Scallion (bright, sharp, savory)

  • 4 green onions, very finely sliced
  • 2 Tbsp fresh ginger, finely grated
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • ¼ cup neutral oil
  • 1 Tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 Tbsp rice vinegar

Sauce C — Honey-Sriracha Soy (sweet heat, no-cook, all pantry)

  • 2 Tbsp soy sauce
  • 1½ Tbsp honey
  • 1 Tbsp sriracha — adjust to taste
  • 1 Tbsp rice vinegar or apple cider vinegar
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • ¼ tsp garlic powder

Mushrooms & spinach

  • 8 oz mushrooms, sliced ¼ inch — shiitake, cremini, or a mix
  • 1 Tbsp neutral oil
  • 3 garlic cloves, thin-sliced
  • 5 oz baby spinach
  • 2 tsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • Pinch of salt

Hot-pan veggie mix (about 1⅓ lb total, cut bite-size and uniform)

  • Hard: 1 head broccoli in small florets; 1 large carrot, thin-sliced on a bias
  • Soft: 1 red bell pepper, strips; 1 cup snap peas, trimmed; ½ red onion, wedges
  • 2 Tbsp neutral oil, divided
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • 1 Tbsp water

Eggs

  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 tsp neutral oil
  • Pinch of salt

To finish

  • 3 green onions, thin-sliced
  • 1 tsp each white and black sesame seeds, toasted

Optional garnishes

  • 1 cup shelled edamame, thawed (more protein)
  • ¼ cup sliced almonds, toasted
  • 1 sheet roasted nori, thin strips
  • Kimchi or quick-pickled cucumbers (from Bulgogi Rice Bowls)

Steps

1. Rice

Start it first. It holds covered while you cook.

2. Sauces

  • Peanut-Lime: Whisk everything except the water. Add warm water a tablespoon at a time until it drizzles. Taste: too thick, more water; too sweet, more lime.
  • Ginger-Scallion: Put green onions, ginger, and salt in a heatproof bowl. Heat the oil in a small pan until it just shimmers and a piece of scallion sizzles hard. Pour over — it will sizzle and bloom. Stir in soy and vinegar. Let sit 10 minutes.
  • Honey-Sriracha Soy: Whisk everything until the honey dissolves. Taste and adjust sriracha.

3. Mushrooms & spinach

  1. Skillet medium-high until hot. Add oil.
  2. Mushrooms in a single layer. Don't stir for 3 minutes. Stir, cook 2–3 minutes more until golden.
  3. Add garlic, 30 seconds, until fragrant.
  4. Pile in spinach. Toss 1–2 minutes just until wilted.
  5. Off heat: soy, sesame oil, taste for salt. Tip into a bowl. Wipe the pan.

4. Hot-pan veggie mix

  1. Pan back over high heat until it just starts to smoke. 1 Tbsp oil.
  2. Hard veggies first. Broccoli and carrots in one layer. Don't stir for 2 minutes — let them char. Toss, add 1 Tbsp water, and let it steam off (about 30 seconds) to finish the broccoli.
  3. Soft veggies next. Push hard veggies to the side, add remaining oil, then pepper, snap peas, and onion. 2–3 minutes, tossing occasionally, until charred in spots and still crisp.
  4. Salt, toss, off heat.

5. Fried eggs

Wipe the pan, medium-high, oil. Crack in eggs; cook 2–3 minutes until whites are set with crisp edges and yolks are runny. Pinch of salt.

6. Build the bowls

Rice → mushroom-spinach on one side → veggie mix on the other → fried egg on top → green onions → sesame seeds → sauces. Set out all three and the optional garnishes.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Salt goes on the vegetables at the end. Salted early, they release water and steam instead of searing. Soy seasons the mushrooms and both sauces.

Fat. A thin film of high-smoke-point oil gets the pan hot enough to char. Peanut butter makes Sauce A rich and creamy. In Sauce B, hot oil blooms the ginger and scallion and carries their flavor through the whole bowl.

Acid. Lime in Sauce A, rice vinegar in Sauce B. A vegetable bowl without acid tastes flat; each sauce brings its own brightness.

Sweet. High heat caramelizes the natural sugars in carrots, peppers, and onions. Honey rounds out the peanut sauce.

Umami. Mushrooms do the heavy lifting a meat would. Seared shiitake are rich in guanylate, which multiplies the glutamate in soy — the same synergy that makes the ramen work.

Heat (temperature). High heat and space in the pan are everything. Crowded vegetables steam and go limp. Hard vegetables go in first because they need more time; soft ones go in last so everything finishes crisp-tender together.

Protein. The fried egg turns a side dish into dinner. The runny yolk works as a fourth sauce, coating the rice.

Variants

  • Crispy tofu: Press 14 oz extra-firm tofu 15 min, cube, toss with 1 Tbsp cornstarch, sear in the hot pan before the veggies until golden on all sides.
  • Different veggies: Zucchini, bok choy, cabbage, green beans, or baby corn. Keep hard and soft groups separate.
  • Fourth sauce: Gochujang drizzle from Bulgogi Rice Bowls.
  • Egg swap: Poached eggs from the ramen recipe, or jammy eggs made ahead.
  • Grain swap: Brown rice, quinoa, or soba noodles.
  • Meal prep: Rice and veggies keep 4 days. Store sauces separately: Peanut-Lime 1 week, Ginger-Scallion 5 days, Honey-Sriracha Soy 2 weeks. Fry eggs fresh.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Veggies soggy Pan not hot enough or too crowded. Cook in two batches
Broccoli still raw Add the water splash and cover 30 seconds
Spinach watery Overcooked. Pull it the moment it wilts
Peanut sauce seized/thick Whisk in warm water, a tablespoon at a time
Ginger-scallion sauce bland Oil wasn't hot enough to bloom it. Needs a hard sizzle
Mushrooms soggy Pan crowded or stirred too early. Leave them alone

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Dry vegetables, hot pan, room to breathe. Same browning rule as meat: water and crowding are the enemies of color.
  • Contrast makes a dish. Rich vs. bright, soft vs. crunchy, mild vs. spicy. Three sauces with three profiles is contrast on purpose.

Notes for the Kids

  • Cut everything before you turn on the stove. Hot-pan cooking moves fast.
  • Hard veggies first, soft veggies last.
  • Don't crowd the pan. Two batches beat one soggy batch.
  • Three sauces, same bowl — you'll find a favorite.
  • No peanut butter or fresh ginger? The honey-sriracha sauce is always there.

Chipotle-Pineapple Shrimp Tacos

Cast-iron seared, with jalapeño crema slaw, pickled onions, mango salsa & a sauce bar

Serves4–6 (12–16 tacos)
Active~1 hr
Total~1.5 hrs (sauces and onions can be made a day ahead)
Equipment12-inch cast iron skillet, paper towels, blender, sheet pan

Game Plan

When What
Day before (or 2 hrs ahead) Pickled onions, sauces, crema
1 hr before Salt cabbage for slaw; make mango salsa
20 min before Dry and season shrimp
10 min before Warm tortillas, dress slaw
Go time Sear shrimp in batches, serve immediately

1. The Shrimp

Shrimp

  • 1½ lb large shrimp (21–25 count), peeled and deveined, tails off

Rub

  • 2–3 Tbsp Kinder's Chipotle Pineapple Seasoning
  • Kosher salt only if needed (see step 2)

To cook and finish

  • 2 Tbsp avocado oil, divided
  • 1 Tbsp butter
  • 1 lime, cut into wedges

Steps

  1. Dry the shrimp. Spread on a sheet pan lined with paper towels, cover with more paper towels, and press. Let sit 5–10 minutes, then pat again. Dry shrimp sear; wet shrimp steam.
  2. Check the rub. Read the label. If salt is the first or second ingredient, it's seasoned enough. If it's salt-free, add 1½ tsp Morton kosher salt — the book's 1% rule (1 tsp Morton per lb).
  3. Season. Toss shrimp with the rub until evenly coated. Season no more than 20–30 minutes before cooking. Sear one test shrimp and taste before cooking the rest; adjust salt or rub.
  4. Heat the pan. Cast iron over high heat 5 minutes, until a drop of water skitters and vanishes instantly. Add 1 Tbsp oil; it should shimmer and just start to smoke.
  5. Sear in batches. Lay shrimp in a single layer with space between — no more than half the shrimp per batch. Don't touch them.
    • First side: 60–90 seconds, until deeply browned at the edges.
    • Flip: 30–60 seconds more.
    • Last 15 seconds of the final batch: add the butter, swirl, and spoon over.
  6. Pull them at the "C." Shrimp are done when opaque and curled into a loose C shape. A tight "O" means overcooked.
  7. Finish with acid. Squeeze lime over the shrimp right off the heat.

2. Jalapeño Crema Slaw & Pickled Onions

Use the recipes from Smoked Pork Butt Sandwiches — same slaw, same onions. Half the slaw recipe is plenty for tacos.

3. Mango Salsa

Yield: ~2 cups | Best within 4 hours

  • 2 ripe mangoes, diced ¼ inch (about 2½ cups)
  • ½ small red bell pepper, finely diced
  • ¼ small red onion, finely diced
  • 1 jalapeño, seeded, minced
  • ⅓ cup cilantro, chopped
  • 2 Tbsp lime juice
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • Pinch of chipotle powder (optional)
  1. Combine everything. Rest 15 minutes so the salt pulls out juice and the flavors blend.
  2. Taste: flat, add salt; too sweet, add lime.

4. Sauce Bar (optional toppings)

A. Chipotle-Lime Crema — smoky, creamy, medium heat

Yield: ~1 cup | Keeps 5 days

  • ½ cup sour cream
  • ¼ cup mayonnaise
  • 1–2 chipotles in adobo + 1 Tbsp adobo sauce
  • Zest of 1 lime + 1 Tbsp juice
  • 1 small garlic clove
  • ¼ tsp kosher salt

Blend until smooth. Thin with water to drizzling consistency. Use a squeeze bottle.

B. Avocado-Tomatillo Salsa Verde — bright, tangy, mild

Yield: ~2 cups | Best same day

  • 8 oz tomatillos (4–5), husked and rinsed
  • 1 ripe avocado
  • 1 serrano or jalapeño, stemmed
  • ½ cup cilantro, stems and leaves
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 2 Tbsp lime juice
  • ¾ tsp kosher salt
  • Water to thin
  1. Simmer tomatillos in water 5 minutes until olive green. Drain. (Or use raw for sharper flavor.)
  2. Blend everything until smooth. Thin with water to pourable.
  3. Press plastic wrap on the surface to keep it green.

C. Charred Pineapple-Habanero Hot Sauce — sweet heat, hot

Yield: ~1½ cups | Keeps 1 month refrigerated

  • ⅓ fresh pineapple (about 10 oz), cut in spears
  • 2–3 habaneros, stemmed (seed for less heat; wear gloves)
  • ½ small white onion, in wedges
  • 2 garlic cloves, unpeeled
  • ½ cup apple cider vinegar
  • 2 tsp honey
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  1. Char pineapple, habaneros, onion, and garlic in the dry cast iron over high heat (or on the grill) until blackened in spots, 6–8 minutes. Peel garlic.
  2. Blend everything until smooth.
  3. Simmer 5 minutes to mellow the raw vinegar edge. Cool. Thin with water if needed.

5. Build the Tacos

  • 16 flour tortillas or fresh corn tortillas (6 inch) — fresh corn from a tortillería or Latin market, not shelf-stable
  • 1 cup (4 oz) queso fresco, crumbled
  • Cilantro sprigs, lime wedges
  1. Warm tortillas.
    • Flour: Dry hot skillet, 20–30 sec per side until puffed with light brown spots.
    • Fresh corn: Directly over a gas flame or in a dry hot skillet, 10–15 sec per side until lightly charred and pliable.
    • Stack in a towel to stay warm and soft.
  2. Build: tortilla → slaw → shrimp (3–4) → mango salsa → pickled onions → cheese.
  3. Set out the sauce bar, lime wedges, and cilantro.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Shrimp are small and absorb salt quickly, so they're seasoned at the last minute — long salting turns them mushy. The 1% rule still applies by weight.

Fat. A thin film of high-smoke-point avocado oil gets the pan hot enough to brown without burning. Butter at the end adds richness. Crema, avocado, and cheese bring creamy fat to balance the spice and acid.

Acid. Lime goes on the shrimp right off the heat, and acid runs through every component: pickled onions, lime in the salsa and crema, vinegar in the hot sauce. Lean shrimp need that brightness.

Sweet. The rub's dried pineapple and sugar caramelize in the fast, hot sear — a little browns beautifully; leave it too long and it burns. Mango salsa adds fresh sweetness to balance chipotle heat.

Umami. The hard sear creates Maillard browning on the shrimp, which are naturally rich in glutamates. Queso fresco adds a mild, salty creaminess.

Heat (temperature). Ripping-hot cast iron browns the outside before the inside overcooks. Shrimp cook in about 2 minutes total — the biggest mistake is crowding the pan, which drops the temperature and steams them.

Spice. Three levels of heat on the table — mild verde, medium chipotle crema, hot habanero — so everyone builds their own.

Variants

  • Charcoal grill: Thread on skewers, grill over high direct heat, 1–2 min per side.
  • Fish tacos: Same rub and toppings on mahi-mahi or snapper. Sear 3–4 min per side.
  • Kid-friendly: Use half the rub; serve with salsa verde.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Shrimp steamed, no browning Pan not hot enough, shrimp wet, or crowded. Dry better, cook smaller batches
Rub burned/bitter Pan smoking too heavily. Pull heat back slightly; wipe pan between batches
Shrimp rubbery Overcooked. Pull at the C shape; they keep cooking off the heat
Corn tortillas crack Not warm enough, or not fresh. Heat until pliable; keep wrapped in a towel
Too salty Rub was salt-heavy. Skip added salt; extra lime and crema balance it
Salsa verde turned brown Air exposure. Press plastic onto the surface; extra lime helps

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Layer your acids. Cooked acid (pickled onions) and raw acid (fresh lime) taste different. Using both gives brightness with depth.
  • Give food room. A crowded pan drops in temperature and releases steam. Space between pieces is what makes a sear.

Notes for the Kids

  • Dry shrimp are the whole secret. Don't skip the paper towels.
  • Two minutes total. Stand at the stove and watch them.
  • C means cooked. O means overdone.
  • Make the sauces the day before and taco night is 15 minutes of work.

Pressure Cooker Carnitas Tacos

Pork shoulder pressure-cooked until tender, then broiled in its own fat until the edges crackle

Serves6–8 (about 16 tacos)
Active25 min
Total~1 hr 30 min (add a day if you dry-brine)
Keeps4 days refrigerated, 3 months frozen
EquipmentPressure cooker, rimmed sheet pan (or 12-inch cast iron), fat separator or spoon, two forks, oven with a broiler
Inspired byIsabel Eats' pressure cooker carnitas, reworked with the book's salt rule and a defatting step

Game Plan

When What
Day before (optional) Cut the pork, salt and rub it. Fridge, uncovered
1 hr 30 min before Cut pork, rub, load the cooker
40 min under pressure + 15 min natural release Make pickled onions and salsa while you wait
20 min before eating Shred, defat the juices, broil
Serve Warm tortillas, build

Ingredients

Pork

  • 3 lb boneless pork shoulder (Boston butt), cut into 3–4 inch chunks, fat left on

Dry brine + rub

  • 1 Tbsp Morton kosher salt — about 1% of the pork's weight
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp dried oregano (Mexican oregano if you have it)
  • 2 tsp chili powder
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp onion powder
  • ½ tsp coarse black pepper

Cooking liquid

  • ½ cup chicken stock
  • ½ cup orange juice
  • 1 bay leaf (optional)

Finish

  • 2 limes: zest of 1, juice of both
  • ⅓ cup cilantro, chopped
  • Pinch of flake salt, only if needed

To build

  • 16 corn or flour tortillas (6 inch)
  • Quick-Pickled Red Onions (from Smoked Pork Butt Sandwiches)
  • Avocado-Tomatillo Salsa Verde and/or Chipotle-Lime Crema (from the Shrimp Tacos sauce bar)
  • Queso fresco, crumbled; cilantro sprigs; lime wedges

Steps

1. Cut and season

  1. Cut into big chunks, 3–4 inches. Large pieces stay juicy; small ones turn to mush under pressure. Don't trim the fat — it's the frying fat later.
  2. Weigh and salt. Weigh the pork. Use 1 tsp Morton per pound. Mix the salt and every spice in the rub.
  3. Coat. Rub into every surface. If you have the time, set on a rack, uncovered, in the fridge overnight — a dry brine seasons through to the center. If not, rub and cook right away.

2. Pressure cook

  1. Pour the stock and orange juice into the cooker. Add the bay leaf.
  2. Nestle the pork in, fat side up, in a snug layer. Pork should sit above the liquid, not drown in it.
  3. High pressure, 40 minutes.
  4. Natural release 15 minutes, then quick-release whatever pressure is left. A natural release keeps the muscle fibers from seizing and squeezing out juice.

3. Shred and defat

  1. Lift the chunks onto a sheet pan, leaving the liquid in the cooker. Remove bay leaf and any big pieces of fat or gristle.
  2. Let the pork cool 5 minutes, then shred with two forks into bite-size pieces — some ragged, some chunky. The ragged edges crisp best.
  3. Skim the liquid. Pour the cooker juices into a fat separator (or let stand 5 minutes and spoon the fat off the top). Keep both the fat and the juices.

4. Crisp

  1. Heat the broiler on high, with the rack 5–6 inches from the element.
  2. Spread the pork on the sheet pan in a thin, even layer. Spoon over ⅓ cup of the defatted juices and 2 Tbsp of the skimmed fat.
  3. Broil 5 minutes, stir, and broil 5 minutes more until the edges are browned and crisp and the middle stays juicy. Watch it from minute 4 — the orange juice sugar browns fast.
  4. Cast iron option: Fry the shredded pork in 2 Tbsp of the skimmed fat over medium-high heat in batches, pressing down and not stirring for 2 minutes at a time, until browned on one side.

5. Finish

Off the heat, toss with the lime zest, lime juice, and cilantro. Taste. Add a pinch of flake salt only if it needs it. Splash a few more spoonfuls of the defatted juices over if it looks dry.

6. Build

Warm the tortillas (flour: dry hot skillet, 20–30 seconds per side; corn: over a flame or in a dry skillet, 10–15 seconds per side). Tortilla → carnitas → pickled onions → salsa verde or crema → queso fresco → cilantro → lime squeeze.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. 1% by weight, worked in ahead of time, seasons the pork all the way through. A last-minute pinch of flake salt is for the crisp edges, not the center.

Fat. Pork shoulder is marbled and has a fat cap, so it becomes tender under pressure. The fat is skimmed and returned on purpose: a spoonful over the pan gives you crisp edges instead of dry ones. That is the real carnitas trick — braise, then fry in the pork's own fat.

Acid. Orange juice in the pot flavors the meat. Lime goes on last, off the heat, so it stays fresh and sharp. Pickled onions add a third layer of acid at the table.

Sweet. Orange juice brings a little sugar that browns at the broiler. This is one of the few times we want sugar near high heat — and only for 10 minutes, with a watchful eye.

Umami. Cumin, oregano, and chili build a savory backbone. Broiling browns the surface (Maillard reaction) so the shreds taste roasted, not just boiled.

Heat (temperature). Pressure does in 40 minutes what an oven needs 3 hours for, melting the collagen into gelatin. Then dry, high heat is the second act: moist cooking makes it tender, dry cooking makes it crisp. You need both.

Variants

  • Smoked first: Smoke the rubbed chunks on the pellet smoker at 225°F for 1 hour, then pressure cook as above. Add 10 minutes of cooking time.
  • Slow cooker: Same rub and liquid, low 8–9 hours. Shred and broil the same way.
  • Pantry shortcut: Replace the rub with 3 Tbsp Kinder's Chipotle Pineapple Seasoning (skip the added salt; it's in the blend).
  • Carnitas bowls: Over rice with beans, pickled onions, and the Avocado-Tomatillo Salsa Verde.
  • Leftovers: Stuff into quesadillas, crisp in a skillet with an egg for breakfast tacos, or stir into the Loaded Packet Ramen.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Meat dry Too little fat added back at the broil. Add juices and skimmed fat
Meat won't shred Not enough pressure time. Reseal and cook 8–10 more minutes
Not crisp Layer too thick, or pan overcrowded. Spread thin; broil in two pans if needed
Burned edges Rack too close or orange juice sugar. Move the rack down; stir sooner
"Burn" warning on the cooker Not enough liquid, or spices stuck to the bottom. Keep the liquid at ½ cup minimum, pork on top, spices on the meat
Too salty The juices concentrate. Use fewer juices at the broil and add extra lime
Soggy tortillas Keep them in a towel; drain the carnitas of excess juice before building

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Wet heat makes it tender; dry heat makes it crisp. Great braised dishes finish with a second, drier cooking step.
  • Fat you removed can come back with a purpose. Skim it for control, then add it back where you want browning.
  • Acid last. Lime added at the end stays bright. Cooked acid goes dull.

Notes for the Kids

  • Big chunks. They look wrong, but they cook up juicy.
  • Let the pressure come down slowly. Rushing it makes tough meat.
  • Don't skip the broil. It's the part that makes it carnitas.
  • Skim the fat, but put a little back. That's how you get crispy edges.
  • Makes a lot. Freeze half in a bag with a spoon of juices.

  • Liquid amounts and pressure time are tuned from the source recipe; test once and adjust.

Smoked Pork Butt Sandwiches

with Carolina Mustard Sauce, Jalapeño Crema Slaw & Quick-Pickled Onions

Serves12–16 sandwiches
Active~1.5 hrs
Total20–26 hrs (plan a day ahead)
EquipmentPellet smoker, oven, cooler, instant-read + leave-in probe thermometer, butcher string, pink butcher paper, heavy foil, aluminum half-pan, 4–6 old towels

Game Plan

When What
Day before, morning Trim, tie, salt + rub. Fridge uncovered 12–24 hrs
Day before, evening Make pickled onions, mustard sauce, crema
Cook day, early (≈ 3–4 AM for dinner) Smoker on at 200°F, pork in
Pork hits 160°F (≈ 9–11 hrs) Baste, wrap in butcher paper, into 210°F oven
Pork hits 190°F (≈ 2–4 hrs) Foil, pan, towel, cooler — carryover finishes it
1 hr before eating Salt cabbage, build slaw
Serving Shred, sauce, assemble

Cook times are guides. Temperature and feel decide when it's done, not the clock.

1. The Pork

Meat

  • 1 pork butt (Boston butt), bone-in or boneless, ~8 lb

Dry brine + rub (house rub)

  • 2½ Tbsp Morton kosher salt — about 1% of meat weight
  • ¼ cup dark brown sugar, packed
  • 3 Tbsp sweet paprika
  • 1½ Tbsp smoked paprika
  • 1 Tbsp coarse black pepper
  • 1 Tbsp granulated garlic
  • 2 tsp onion powder
  • 2 tsp dry mustard
  • ½ tsp cayenne

Vinegar-sugar baste

  • 1 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 2 Tbsp dark brown sugar
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tsp red pepper flakes

Pellets: Competition blend (typically hickory, maple, and cherry).

Steps

  1. Trim and dry. Pat dry all over with paper towels. Trim the fat cap to ¼ inch. Remove hard fat and silverskin. Leave the soft interior fat — it renders and keeps the meat moist.
  2. Weigh and salt. Weigh the trimmed butt. If it isn't 8 lb, scale the salt: 1 tsp Morton per pound.
  3. Tie. Run butcher string around the butt every 1½–2 inches with a snug knot. You want a compact, even shape that cooks evenly — not a tourniquet.
  4. Dry brine + rub. Mix salt and rub spices. Coat every surface, pressing it in. Set on a rack over a sheet pan, uncovered, in the fridge 12–24 hours.
  5. Make the baste. Warm vinegar, sugar, salt, and pepper flakes until dissolved. Cool. Put about half in a spray bottle; keep the rest for wrapping.
  6. Smoke. Fill the hopper (8+ hours of pellets — top off before bed if running overnight). Bring the pellet smoker to 200°F; low temps give the most smoke on a pellet grill. If yours has a "Super Smoke" mode, use it for the first 3–4 hours. Pork in fat-side up, leave-in probe in the thickest part, away from the bone. Don't open the lid for the first 3 hours.
  7. Spritz. After 3 hours, spritz every 60–90 minutes when the surface looks dry. Expect the stall around 150–165°F — temperature can sit flat for hours. This is normal: moisture is evaporating off the surface and cooling it.
  8. Wrap at 160°F. Bark should be deep mahogany and set (doesn't smear when touched). Lay out two overlapping sheets of butcher paper. Brush the pork all over with the reserved baste and pour 2–3 Tbsp over the top. Wrap tight.
  9. Oven finish. Into a 210°F oven, probe still in. Pull at 190°F.
  10. Hold. The pork keeps climbing in the cooler and sits in the 180–190s for hours — that long, gentle hold finishes melting the collagen. Leave the paper on. Wrap in heavy foil, set in the aluminum pan, wrap the pan in a towel. Put in a dry cooler and pack the gaps with towels. Close.
    • Minimum rest: 2 hours.
    • Safe hold: up to 4 hours. Internal temp should stay above 140°F.
  11. Shred. Unwrap over the pan to catch juices. Remove the string. If bone-in, the bone should slide clean out. Shred with forks or gloved hands, discarding large fat pockets. Toss with the pan juices, then taste and add a pinch of salt or a splash of baste if needed.

2. Carolina Mustard Sauce

Yield: ~2 cups | Keeps 3 weeks refrigerated

  • 1 cup yellow mustard
  • ½ cup apple cider vinegar
  • ¼ cup dark brown sugar, packed
  • 2 Tbsp honey
  • 1 Tbsp Worcestershire
  • 1 Tbsp unsalted butter
  • 1–2 tsp hot sauce
  • 1 tsp coarse black pepper
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  1. Whisk everything except the butter in a small saucepan.
  2. Simmer gently 8–10 minutes, stirring, until slightly thickened. Keep heat low — the sugar scorches.
  3. Off heat, stir in the butter. Taste: too sharp, add honey; too flat, add vinegar or a pinch of salt.
  4. Best made a day ahead.

3. Jalapeño Crema Slaw

Yield: ~8 cups

Crema

  • ½ cup sour cream
  • ½ cup mayonnaise
  • 2–3 jalapeños, seeded for mild, minced
  • ½ cup loosely packed cilantro, chopped
  • Zest of 1 lime + 2 Tbsp juice
  • 1 small garlic clove, grated
  • 1 tsp honey
  • ½ tsp kosher salt

Slaw

  • ½ medium head green cabbage (about 1½ lb), thin-sliced
  • ¼ small head red cabbage (about 7 oz), thin-sliced
  • 1 large carrot, julienned or coarse-grated
  • 2 tsp Morton kosher salt — about 1% of cabbage weight
  • 3 scallions, thin-sliced
  1. Crema. Blend or whisk everything. Chill at least 30 minutes so the jalapeño heat blooms.
  2. Salt the cabbage. Toss cabbage and carrot with the salt. Sit in a colander 1 hour. Rinse quickly, then squeeze hard in a towel. This pulls out water so the slaw stays crunchy and the dressing doesn't thin out.
  3. Dress. Toss with crema and scallions. Start with ¾ of the crema and add to taste. Serve within 2 hours.

4. Quick-Pickled Red Onions

Yield: 1 pint | Ready in 1 hour, best after 1 day | Keeps 3 weeks

  • 2 medium red onions, sliced ⅛ inch
  • ½ cup red wine vinegar or apple cider vinegar
  • ½ cup water
  • 1 Tbsp sugar
  • 1½ tsp kosher salt
  • Optional: 1 tsp black peppercorns, 1 bay leaf, 1 smashed garlic clove
  1. Pack onions into a pint jar.
  2. Bring vinegar, water, sugar, salt, and aromatics to a boil.
  3. Pour hot over onions, pressing them under. Cool on the counter, then refrigerate.

5. Build the Sandwich

  • 12–16 Hawaiian sweet rolls or buns
  • Butter for toasting
  1. Split and butter the buns. Toast cut-side down in a hot skillet until golden.
  2. Bottom bun → pork → drizzle of mustard sauce → slaw → pickled onions → top bun.
  3. Extra sauce on the table.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. 1% salt as a dry brine penetrates over 12–24 hours, seasoning deep into the meat and helping it hold moisture. The same 1% on cabbage pulls water out so the slaw stays crisp.

Fat. Pork butt is heavily marbled with collagen and fat. Low heat gives the collagen time to melt into gelatin — that's what makes it juicy and shreddable. Crema and mayo add a cool, creamy fat to balance the rich pork.

Acid. Acid shows up at every layer: vinegar baste, mustard sauce, lime crema, pickled onions. Each one cuts the pork's richness. Acid goes on late — at the wrap, at the sauce, at the table.

Sweet. Brown sugar in the rub builds bark; honey in the sauce rounds off vinegar's edge; Hawaiian buns echo it. All sugar stays at low temperatures (200°F), so it caramelizes without burning.

Umami. Long smoke and slow browning (Maillard reaction) create savory depth. Worcestershire in the sauce adds a fermented boost.

Heat (temperature). 200°F smoke maximizes smoke time and bark. Wrapping at 160°F pushes through the stall. A 210°F oven gives steady heat without burning pellets. Pulling at 190°F and holding in the cooler uses carryover heat to finish the collagen breakdown gently.

Variants

  • Pressure cooker shortcut: Rub, smoke 3 hours for flavor, then pressure cook 75 min with ½ cup baste, natural release. Less bark, same flavor profile.
  • Charcoal grill: Set up for indirect heat (snake method) at 225°F with wood chunks.
  • Boneless butt: Tying matters more here. Cook time drops 10–20%.
  • More smoke flavor: Add a pellet smoke tube for the first few hours.
  • Heat level: Leave jalapeño seeds in the crema, or add a minced serrano.

Problem Fix
Stuck in stall for 4+ hrs Normal. Wrap earlier at 155°F if timing is tight
Won't shred Rest was too short, or probe was in a cold spot. Back in the 210°F oven, wrapped, 45–60 min
Pellets ran out overnight Check the probe; if above 140°F, refill and keep going
Bark is soggy Butcher paper breathes; foil doesn't. Don't skip the paper
Pork dried out Toss with pan juices and a splash of baste

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Salt early for big cuts. Salt moves into meat slowly. The thicker the cut, the earlier it needs salt — a day ahead for a pork butt seasons it to the center.
  • Match heat to the cut. Tough, collagen-rich cuts want low heat and lots of time; tender cuts want high heat and little time.

Smoked Pork Ribs

Smoked, paper-wrapped, oven-finished, flame-glazed

Serves4–6 (2 racks)
Active~1 hr
Total6–8 hrs + overnight dry brine
EquipmentPellet smoker, oven, charcoal grill, probe thermometer, pink butcher paper, butter knife, paper towels, sheet pan with rack

Game Plan

When What
Day before Remove membrane, salt + rub. Fridge uncovered 12–24 hrs
Day before Make glaze
Cook day, ~7 hrs before eating Smoker on at 225°F, ribs in
Ribs hit 160°F (≈ 3–4 hrs) Baste, wrap in butcher paper, into 210°F oven
Ribs hit 195°F (≈ 1.5–2.5 hrs) Out of the oven; light the grill
Grill ready Sauce and sear 6–10 min total
Off the grill Rest 10 min, slice between bones

1. The Ribs

Meat

  • 2 racks St. Louis–cut spare ribs, ~3 lb each (about 6 lb total)

Dry brine + rub (house rub — same as Smoked Pork Butt)

  • 2 Tbsp Morton kosher salt — about 1% of meat weight
  • 3 Tbsp dark brown sugar, packed
  • 2 Tbsp sweet paprika
  • 1 Tbsp smoked paprika
  • 2 tsp coarse black pepper
  • 2 tsp granulated garlic
  • 1½ tsp onion powder
  • 1½ tsp dry mustard
  • ¼ tsp cayenne

Vinegar-sugar baste

  • ½ cup apple cider vinegar
  • 1 Tbsp dark brown sugar
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • ½ tsp red pepper flakes

Pellets: Competition blend.

Steps

  1. Remove the membrane. Bone side up. Slide a butter knife under the thin, shiny membrane at one end. Lift a corner, grab it with a paper towel, and pull it off in one sheet. If it tears, start a new corner.
  2. Trim and dry. Cut off any loose flap of meat and hard fat — thin, hanging bits burn. Pat both sides dry with paper towels so the rub sticks and the bark forms.
  3. Weigh and salt. 1 tsp Morton per pound. Mix with the rub spices.
  4. Dry brine + rub. Coat both sides, heavier on the meat side. On a rack over a sheet pan, uncovered, in the fridge 12–24 hours.
  5. Make the baste. Warm to dissolve, cool. Half in a spray bottle, half reserved for wrapping.
  6. Smoke. Pellet smoker at 225°F. Ribs in, meat side up. Probe in the thickest meat between bones, not touching bone. Use Super Smoke for the first 2 hours if you have it.
  7. Spritz. After 2 hours, spritz every 45–60 minutes when the surface looks dry.
  8. Wrap at 160°F. Bark should be set and mahogany. One rack per sheet of butcher paper, meat side down. Brush with reserved baste, pour 1–2 Tbsp over, wrap tight.
  9. Oven. 210°F, probe in. Pull at 195°F.
  10. Grill finish. Two-zone fire: coals on one side, ~400–450°F over the coals. Unwrap the ribs.
    • Bone side down over direct heat, 1–2 min to firm up.
    • Brush glaze on the meat side, flip meat side down over direct heat, 1–2 min until it bubbles and spots dark. Don't walk away.
    • Flip, glaze again, move to the indirect side, lid on, 3–5 min to set.
    • Repeat a third coat if you want it sticky. Target ~200–203°F and the bend test.
  11. Rest and slice. Rest 10 minutes meat side down. Slice between bones with a long knife.

The bend test

Pick up the rack with tongs about ⅓ in from one end. It should sag deeply and the surface should crack. If it barely bends, it needs more time. If it breaks apart, it's overcooked (still delicious).

  • ¼ cup dark brown sugar, packed
  • 2 Tbsp molasses
  • 1 Tbsp honey
  • 1 Tbsp Worcestershire
  • 1 Tbsp yellow mustard
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp coarse black pepper
  • ½ tsp granulated garlic
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • 2 chipotles in adobo, minced, + 1 Tbsp adobo sauce
  • 1 Tbsp hot sauce (Frank's or similar)
  • ½–1 tsp cayenne

  1. Whisk everything in a small saucepan.
  2. Simmer low 15–20 minutes, stirring, until it coats a spoon thickly. Thick glaze sticks to the ribs instead of dripping into the fire.
  3. Taste: too sweet, add vinegar; too sharp, add honey; too hot, add honey and a little ketchup. Heat builds as it sits, so taste again the next day.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. 1% overnight dry brine seasons through and helps the meat hold moisture over a long cook.

Fat. Spare ribs have plenty of fat and collagen between the bones. Low heat renders the fat and turns collagen into gelatin.

Acid. Vinegar baste at the wrap and vinegar in the glaze cut the richness. Mustard adds a second, sharper acid.

Spice. Chipotle brings smoky heat that echoes the smoker; cayenne and hot sauce add sharper heat. Sugar and fat round off the burn.

Sweet. The rub's brown sugar builds bark at low smoker heat. Glaze sugars go on at the very end on purpose — the one place we want sugar on high heat, briefly. Minutes, not hours.

Umami. Smoke, Maillard browning, tomato, and Worcestershire stack savory depth.

Heat (temperature). 225°F sets bark faster on thinner ribs. Paper-wrapping pushes through the stall without steaming off the bark. Pulling at 195°F leaves room for the grill to finish. Direct heat hits the glaze for a crust you can't get in an oven or smoker.

Variants

  • Baby back ribs: Smaller racks (~2 lb). Smoke to 160°F (≈ 2–2.5 hrs), oven ~1–1.5 hrs.
  • Mild glaze for the kids: Skip the chipotles, hot sauce, and pepper flakes; use ¼ tsp cayenne.
  • Mustard glaze: Swap in the Carolina Mustard Sauce.
  • Dry ribs (Memphis style): Skip the glaze. Dust with rub on the grill instead.
  • No charcoal: Finish under the oven broiler, 3–4 inches from the element, 2–3 min per coat.

  • Browning is flavor. Dry surfaces brown; wet surfaces steam.

Dutch Oven Country-Style Pork Ribs

Seared, braised low and slow, then finished dry, BBQ-glazed, or in a red wine sauce

Serves4–6 (about 3 lb)
Active30 min
Total3½–4 hrs (1 hr rub time included)
EquipmentEnameled Dutch oven (5–7 qt) with lid, wood or silicone tools, instant-read thermometer, fat separator or spoon, rimmed sheet pan

Pick Your Finish

A. Dry (Memphis-style) B. BBQ Glaze C. Red Wine Sauce
Flavor Savory, spice crust, pure pork Sweet, tangy, sticky Deep, rich, wine-braised
Extra work None Make or warm the glaze Reduce the pot juices
Best with Anything; vinegar splash at the table Slaw, green beans, corn Rosemary potatoes, mash, polenta

The seasoning, sear, and braise are the same for all three. Only the last 20 minutes change.

Ingredients

Meat

  • 3 lb bone-in or boneless country-style pork ribs

Dry brine + rub (house rub)

  • 1 Tbsp Morton kosher salt — 1 tsp per pound
  • 1 Tbsp dark brown sugar, packed
  • 1 Tbsp sweet paprika
  • 1½ tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp coarse black pepper
  • 1 tsp granulated garlic
  • ½ tsp onion powder
  • ½ tsp dry mustard
  • Pinch of cayenne

Sear and braise

  • 2 Tbsp avocado oil
  • 1 large yellow onion, sliced
  • 4 garlic cloves, smashed
  • ¼ cup apple cider vinegar
  • Liquid, choose one: Standard: ¾ cup chicken stock (or apple cider) · Red wine option (C): ½ cup dry red wine + ¼ cup chicken stock
  • 2 sprigs fresh thyme or rosemary (optional)

Finish, choose one

  • A. Dry: Splash of cider vinegar and a squeeze of lemon at the table.
  • B. BBQ glaze: ¾–1 cup Spicy House Red Glaze or Carolina Mustard Sauce.
  • C. Red wine sauce: the pot juices, plus 1 tsp Dijon, 1 Tbsp cold butter, and 1–2 tsp red wine vinegar.

Steps

1. Season (an hour ahead, or the night before)

  1. Pat the ribs completely dry. Dry meat browns; wet meat steams.
  2. Weigh and salt at 1 tsp Morton per pound. Mix with the rest of the rub and coat every surface.
  3. Rest at room temperature at least 1 hour, or uncovered on a rack in the fridge overnight.

2. Sear

  1. Heat the pot to medium-high. Not high — a screaming-hot empty enameled pot can craze the coating. Add the oil and let it shimmer.
  2. Brown in batches. Single layer with space between. Leave alone 3–4 minutes until a deep brown crust forms and they release, then turn. About 10–12 minutes total. Move to a plate.
  3. Pour off all but 1 Tbsp of fat. Dark brown bits (fond) are flavor. If they're black, wipe out and use fresh oil.

3. Build the braise

  1. Add onion and garlic. Stir 2–3 minutes until they pick up color and loosen the fond.
  2. Pour in the vinegar and scrape the bottom until the bits dissolve. Simmer 1 minute.
  3. Add the liquid. If using wine, simmer 3 minutes so the alcohol cooks off.
  4. Nestle the ribs back in. Liquid should come about one-third up the sides. Tuck in the herbs.

4. Braise

  1. Bring to a bare simmer, cover, and move to a 300°F oven.
  2. 2–2½ hours. Turn once at 1 hour. Ready when a fork slides in with no resistance and the thickest piece reads 195–200°F. Not tender yet? Cover and give it 20 more minutes.

5. Finish

A. Dry (Memphis-style): Uncover, 425°F for 15–20 minutes until the rub darkens into a crust. Optional thin coat of defatted pot juices at 10 minutes. Rest 10 minutes. Vinegar or lemon at the table.

B. BBQ glaze: Ribs onto a foil-lined sheet pan. Oven to 425°F (or broil, rack 5–6 inches down). Brush a thick coat of glaze. Roast 8 minutes, brush again, 5–8 minutes more until bubbling and spotty dark. Don't walk away. Rest 10 minutes.

C. Red wine sauce: Ribs to a plate, tented. Defat the pot juices, return to the pot with the Dijon, and reduce over medium-high 8–10 minutes by about half. Off the heat, swirl in cold butter. Add red wine vinegar a teaspoon at a time until bright, not sharp. Return ribs, turn to coat, rest 5–10 minutes.

Pair With

  • Hot-Pan Blistered Green Beans
  • Rosemary potatoes: parboil 8 minutes in salted water, drain, shake rough, toss with rosemary oil and flake salt, roast at 425°F 30–35 minutes.
  • Jalapeño Crema Slaw (with the BBQ glaze)
  • Mashed potatoes or polenta (with the red wine sauce)
  • A fruity, low-tannin red: Zinfandel, Grenache, Syrah, or chilled Beaujolais

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. 1% by weight, an hour to a day ahead, seasons through to the center and helps the pork hold moisture.

Fat. Country-style ribs come from the shoulder end of the loin, with plenty of marbling and connective tissue. Slow heat melts collagen into gelatin. Skimming at the end keeps the sauce clean.

Acid. Vinegar in the deglaze cuts the richness. In option C, red wine adds a second layer and a splash of vinegar at the end brightens the sauce.

Sweet. Brown sugar in the rub, softened onions, and glaze sugar balance the acid. Sugar sees high heat only briefly, at the end.

Umami. The sear, the fond, onions, and tomato and Worcestershire in the glaze build depth.

Heat (temperature). Sear hot for flavor, braise low and slow for tenderness, finish at 425°F for crust. Wet heat for tenderness; dry heat for texture.

Variants

  • Pressure cooker shortcut: Sear, add ½ cup liquid and vinegar, high pressure 35–40 minutes, natural release. Finish as above.
  • Slow cooker: Sear in a skillet first, then 6–7 hours on low. Finish in the oven.
  • Beef short ribs: Same method; braise 3–3½ hours.
  • Add vegetables: Carrots and potatoes in the pot for the last hour.
  • Smoke first: 1 hour at 225°F on the pellet smoker, then into the pot.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Ribs tough Under-braised. Cover and cook 20–30 more minutes
Dry Too little liquid, or lid not tight. Keep it one-third up the sides
Gray, no crust Pot not hot enough, meat wet, or crowded
Fond burned Pot too hot. Medium-high only; deglaze before it blackens
Sauce too thin Reduce longer. Too thick: add a spoon of stock
Sauce greasy Not defatted. Skim again
Sauce too sharp Simmer longer; add a pinch of sugar or a pat of butter
Chips in the coating Wood, silicone, or nylon tools only. No empty pot on high heat

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Braise = sear + liquid + time. The same method works for short ribs, chicken thighs, lamb shanks, and pork shoulder.
  • Finish with acid. A braise cooked for hours can taste flat. A little vinegar or lemon at the end fixes that.
  • Separate the fat before you sauce. Fat carries flavor, but too much makes a sauce heavy.

Notes for the Kids

  • Brown the ribs well. That crust turns into the sauce.
  • Medium-high, not high, in an enameled pot. Use wood or silicone tools.
  • Check tenderness with a fork, not the clock.
  • Dry, glazed, or wine sauce: all three are good. Pick by mood.

Dutch Oven Beef Stew

Day-ahead salted chuck, hard sear, red wine, low oven braise, finished with vinegar and butter

Serves6
Active50 min
Total~4 hrs + overnight salt
Equipment5–6 qt Dutch oven with lid, rack and sheet pan, wooden spoon, instant-read thermometer

The Short Version

Salt the beef the day before. Brown it hard in batches. Build a dark aromatic base in the same fat. Deglaze with wine and reduce. Braise at a bare simmer until a fork meets no resistance. Adjust acid and salt at the very end.

Ingredients

Beef

  • 3 lb beef chuck roast, hard fat trimmed, cut into 1½–2 inch cubes
  • 1 Tbsp Morton kosher salt — 1% of meat weight (1 tsp per pound)
  • 3 Tbsp neutral oil, for searing

Aromatic base

  • 2 medium yellow onions, medium dice
  • 2 medium carrots, cut in ¾-inch coins (first half — see "added late")
  • 2 celery stalks, medium dice
  • 8 oz cremini mushrooms, quartered
  • 6 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2½ Tbsp tomato paste
  • 4 anchovy fillets, minced (or 1 Tbsp fish sauce)
  • 1 tsp Morton kosher salt

Liquid

  • 1½ cups dry red wine — something with tannin and acid, not a jammy fruit bomb
  • 3 cups low-sodium beef stock
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 6 sprigs fresh thyme

Added late

  • 2 medium carrots, cut in 1-inch chunks
  • 1 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, 1½-inch chunks
  • 1 tsp Morton kosher salt

Finish

  • 2–3 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 Tbsp cold butter
  • Flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • Black pepper

Steps

1. Salt the day before (40 minutes minimum)

Pat the cubes dry. Toss with the 1 Tbsp salt. Spread on a rack over a sheet pan, uncovered, in the fridge overnight. The salt seasons through and helps the meat hold water; the surface dries out so it browns instead of steams.

2. Before you cook

Pull the beef 45 minutes ahead. Blot every cube dry again. Prep all vegetables. Open the wine. Oven to 275°F, rack in the lower third.

3. Sear — the flavor foundation

  1. Heat 2 Tbsp oil in the Dutch oven over medium-high until it shimmers.
  2. Beef in a single layer with space between cubes — 3 or 4 batches. Don't crowd. Don't stir.
  3. Leave each side 3–4 minutes until deep brown, not tan. Turn and brown a second face. Two good faces per cube is enough.
  4. Move each batch to a bowl. If the fond goes from brown toward black between batches, add a splash of stock and scrape.

4. Build the base

  1. Lower heat to medium. Add the remaining oil if the pot looks dry.
  2. Onion, first-half carrots, celery, and mushrooms with 1 tsp salt. Cook 10–12 minutes, scraping, until the mushrooms have released their water and everything is browned at the edges.
  3. Push the vegetables aside. Add tomato paste and anchovy to the bare spot. Stir 2–3 minutes until the paste darkens to brick red and the anchovy melts away. Add garlic for the last minute.

5. Deglaze

Pour in the wine and scrape every brown bit off the bottom. Simmer hard 5–6 minutes until reduced by about half and no longer smells boozy.

6. Braise

  1. Return the beef and any juices. Add stock, bay, and thyme. Liquid should come about two-thirds up the meat, not cover it.
  2. Bring to a bare simmer on the stove. Cover and move to the 275°F oven.
  3. 2 hours. The liquid should barely smile — a bubble here and there, never a boil.

7. Add the late vegetables

Stir in the late carrots, potatoes, and 1 tsp salt. Cover and braise 45–60 minutes more until the beef gives no resistance to a fork and the potatoes are tender.

8. Finish

  1. Fish out the bay and thyme stems. Skim fat off the top.
  2. Too thin? Simmer uncovered on the stove 10 minutes.
  3. Off the heat: swirl in the cold butter. Add red wine vinegar 1 tsp at a time, tasting after each, until the stew tastes bright, not heavy. Adjust salt.
  4. Parsley and black pepper on top.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Salt in three places — on the beef a day ahead, in the vegetables, with the late additions — so every component is seasoned on its own. Final adjustment comes last, after reduction.

Fat. Chuck is marbled with fat and collagen. Low heat melts the collagen into gelatin, which gives the gravy body. Cold butter at the end adds gloss and roundness.

Acid. Acid three times: wine during the braise, tomato paste in the base, and vinegar off the heat. The last splash wakes up hours of slow cooking.

Sweet. Browned onions and carrots bring natural sweetness. Reducing the wine concentrates its fruit.

Umami. Anchovy + mushrooms + tomato paste + seared beef stack glutamate and nucleotides — they multiply each other. The anchovy disappears completely; nobody will taste fish.

Heat (temperature). 275°F, not 325°F. Even at 300°F a covered pot can boil, which tightens muscle fiber and wrings out juice. A bare simmer melts collagen without drying the meat.

Variants

  • Pressure cooker: Sear and build the base on sauté. Add liquid (reduce stock to 2 cups). High pressure 35 minutes, natural release 15. Add potatoes and late carrots, high pressure 5 minutes, quick release. Finish as above.
  • Slow cooker: Sear and build the base on the stove, then low 8 hours. Add potatoes and late carrots for the last 2 hours.
  • Smoker: Smoke the salted cubes at 225°F for 1 hour before searing for a smoky stew.
  • Outdoor charcoal Dutch oven: Coals under and on the lid, roughly twice as many on top as underneath, refreshed every hour. Keep it at a bare simmer.
  • No wine: Replace with more stock plus 2 Tbsp red wine vinegar added with the stock.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Beef tough Not done yet. Give it 20–30 more minutes covered
Beef dry and stringy Boiled instead of simmered. Lower the oven next time
Gravy thin Simmer uncovered 10–15 minutes, or mash a few potato chunks into it
Tastes flat Needs acid. Add vinegar 1 tsp at a time
Too salty Add potatoes and a splash of water; skip extra salt
Gray meat, no crust Wet beef or crowded pot. Dry it and sear in smaller batches

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Season in layers. Salt each component as it goes in, then adjust at the end.
  • Acid twice. Some during the cook for depth, a splash at the end for brightness.
  • A braise should barely bubble. Gentle heat makes tender meat; boiling makes tough meat.

Notes for the Kids

  • Salt the beef the night before. It's the easiest upgrade in the book.
  • Brown, don't gray. Small batches.
  • The anchovy is a secret. Don't tell anyone and they'll never know.
  • Taste at the end and add vinegar a little at a time. That's the step most people skip.
  • It's better tomorrow. Make it a day ahead if you can.

Grilled Crispy-Skin Chicken Thighs, Two Flavors

Charcoal-grilled bone-in thighs, half tossed in garlic Parmesan, half in honey mustard

Serves4–6 (8 thighs)
Active30 min
Total1 hr + overnight dry brine (optional but best)
EquipmentCharcoal grill, chimney starter, tongs, instant-read thermometer, sheet pan with rack, 2 large mixing bowls

Ingredients

Chicken

  • 8 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (~3 lb)

Dry brine

  • 1 Tbsp Morton kosher salt — 1% of meat weight
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 2 tsp baking powder (aluminum-free) — baking powder, not baking soda. Soda tastes soapy and metallic on skin.

Flavor 1 — Garlic Parmesan

  • ½ cup store-bought garlic Parmesan wing sauce

Flavor 2 — Honey Mustard

  • ½ cup Chick-fil-A sauce or Ken's honey mustard

Steps

Prep (day before is best)

  1. Trim. Trim any skin or fat hanging well past the meat — it's flare-up fuel.
  2. Dry thoroughly. Pat bone-dry with paper towels. Dry skin crisps; wet skin steams.
  3. Dry brine. Mix salt, pepper, garlic powder, paprika, and baking powder. The baking powder raises the skin's pH so it browns faster and dries into tiny crisp bubbles. Season all sides, more on the skin.
  4. Air-dry. Skin up on a rack over a sheet pan, uncovered, in the fridge 4–24 hours. (No time? Season and grill right away; still good.)

Grill

  1. Two-zone fire. Full chimney of charcoal, all on one side. Clean and oil the grates. Lid on to preheat, vents open, 10 minutes.
  2. Render on indirect. Thighs skin side up on the cool side, thickest part toward the coals. Lid on, top vent over the chicken. 25–35 minutes until 155–160°F.
  3. Crisp on direct. Move thighs skin side down over the coals. 2–4 minutes, lid open, until skin is deep golden and crackly. Stay at the grill.
  4. Watch for flare-ups. If flames lick the chicken, slide that piece to the cool side for 30 seconds. Don't spray with water.
  5. Finish. Flip skin up. Pull at 175–185°F near the bone. (Safe at 165°F, but thighs are juicier at 175+.)
  6. Rest 5 minutes on a rack, skin up — not on a plate, where the skin sits in steam.

Toss — last minute

  1. Two bowls while the chicken rests, sauces at room temp (cold sauce chills the chicken).
  2. Toss at the last possible minute. Half the thighs into each bowl, toss, platter, serve.

Pair With

  • Chilled Lemon Asparagus
  • Hot-Pan Blistered Green Beans
  • Chilled Lentils with Shallot Vinaigrette
  • Pickled Beet & Burrata Salad

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. A 1% dry brine overnight seasons deep and pulls moisture out of the skin. Both sauces are salty, so no salt at the end.

Fat. Chicken skin is mostly fat. Slow indirect heat renders it out first, leaving thin, crisp skin.

Acid. Mustard brings sharp acid. Add a squeeze of lemon to the garlic Parm thighs if they taste heavy.

Sweet. Honey balances the mustard. Sugar burns on the grill, which is why sauces go on after cooking.

Umami. Charred, rendered skin and the Parmesan in the wing sauce.

Heat (temperature). Two zones solve flare-ups. Indirect heat cooks and renders away from the coals; direct heat comes last and fast, only to crisp.

Variants

  • Pellet smoker: 375°F skin up, 35–45 minutes to 175°F, then 2 minutes over direct charcoal (or under the broiler) to crisp.
  • Oven: 425°F on a rack, skin up, 35–45 minutes.
  • Other flavors: Any store wing sauce — Buffalo, lemon pepper, sweet chili.
  • Boneless thighs: Skip indirect; 4–5 minutes per side over medium direct heat.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Rubbery skin Not dried enough, or not enough indirect time. Dry-brine uncovered overnight
Constant flare-ups Trim overhang; render longer on indirect first
Burnt skin, raw inside Started on direct heat. Always indirect first, direct last
Skin soggy after tossing Tossed too early
Soapy or metallic taste Baking soda was used instead of baking powder

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Salt a day ahead when you can. Early salt seasons deeply and dries the skin — two jobs at once.
  • Use more than one heat. Gentle heat to cook through, intense heat to brown.

Notes for the Kids

  • Dry the skin. Then dry it again.
  • Cool side first, hot side last. That's the whole trick.
  • Don't walk away during the crisp. Thirty seconds goes from golden to burnt.
  • Thighs at 175°F beat breasts at 165°F every time.
  • Baking powder, not baking soda. Check the can.

BBQ Wings, Two Ways

Smoked and reverse-seared, or grill-only (with an oven finish option)

Serves4 (about 3 lb wings)
Active30 min
Total1.5 hrs + overnight dry brine
EquipmentCharcoal grill, chimney starter, tongs, instant-read thermometer, sheet pan with rack, large bowl. Method A: pellet smoker.

Pick Your Method

A. Smoke + Reverse Sear B. Grill Only / Grill to Oven
Flavor Deep smoke, crisp char Charcoal char, quicker
Time ~1.5 hrs ~45 min
Equipment Pellet smoker + charcoal grill Charcoal grill (+ oven option)
Best for Weekends, bigger batches Weeknights, fewer tools

Ingredients

Wings

  • 3 lb chicken wings, split into flats and drums, tips removed (save for stock)

Dry brine

  • 1 Tbsp Morton kosher salt — 1% of meat weight
  • 1 Tbsp baking powder (aluminum-free) — powder, not soda
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika

Sauce

  • ¾ cup store-bought BBQ sauce — whatever fits your mood
  • 1 Tbsp butter, melted — loosens the sauce for tossing

Prep (both methods)

  1. Dry. Pat wings completely dry with paper towels.
  2. Dry brine. Mix salt, baking powder, and spices. Toss wings until evenly coated.
  3. Air-dry. Single layer on a rack over a sheet pan, uncovered, in the fridge 8–24 hours (minimum 2).

Method A — Pellet Smoker + Reverse Sear

  1. Smoke. 250°F, competition blend pellets. 45–60 minutes to 160°F. Skin will be golden but soft — expected.
  2. Fire the grill. Full chimney, two-zone fire.
  3. Reverse sear. Wings on the direct side, 2–3 minutes per side, lid open, turning often until crisp and charred in spots.
  4. Done: 175–190°F.

Method B — Grill Only

  1. Two-zone fire, preheat to ~375–400°F.
  2. Indirect. Wings on the cool side, lid on, 25–30 minutes, flipping once, to 165°F.
  3. Crisp direct. 2–3 minutes per side, lid open. Stay close.
  4. Done: 175–190°F.

  1. Warm sauce and melted butter in a small pan until loose. Pour into a large bowl.
  2. Toss at the last minute. Wings in, toss, platter, serve.

Salt. 1% dry brine seasons the meat and draws moisture out of the skin overnight.

Fat. Wings are mostly skin and fat. Low heat first renders it; high heat last crisps what's left.

Acid. Vinegar in the BBQ sauce cuts the rich skin.

Sweet. BBQ sauce sugar burns fast over coals, so it goes on after cooking.

Umami. Smoke, Maillard char, tomato and Worcestershire in the sauce.

Heat (temperature). Reverse sear = low and slow first, high and fast last. Backward burns the outside before the fat renders.

Baking powder. Raises the skin's pH so it browns faster and dries into tiny crisp bubbles — fried-wing texture without the fryer.

  • Dry rub wings: Skip the sauce. Dust with the house rub right off the grill.

  • Set the glaze: After tossing, 30–60 seconds back over direct heat. Watch closely.

Soggy after saucing Tossed too early
Wings stuck to grates Grates not clean, oiled, or hot
Soapy taste Baking soda instead of baking powder

  • Sweet goes on late. Cook first, sauce after.

Basic Smash Burgers

Good beef, weighed balls, a screaming-hot griddle

Serves4 (double-patty burgers)
Active20 min
Total25 min
EquipmentCast iron griddle or 12-inch skillet, stiff metal spatula with a sharp edge, second spatula or press, parchment squares, kitchen scale

The Three Rules

  1. Meat quality is the burger. Smash burgers are mostly crust and beef — there's nowhere for bad meat to hide.
  2. Weigh every ball. Same weight = same thickness = same cook time.
  3. The griddle must be ripping hot. 450–500°F surface, or the meat steams gray.

Ingredients

Beef

  • 1½ lb 80/20 ground chuck, freshly ground, kept cold

Fry sauce

  • ½ cup mayonnaise
  • ¼ cup ketchup
  • 1 tsp pickle brine
  • ½ tsp garlic powder
  • ¼ tsp onion powder
  • ¼ tsp smoked paprika
  • Pinch of black pepper
  • Hot variant: 1–2 tsp hot sauce

Build

  • 4 potato buns
  • 1 Tbsp butter, softened
  • 8 slices American cheese
  • ½ small white onion, sliced paper-thin
  • Dill pickle chips
  • Kosher salt and fresh-ground black pepper

The Meat

Best: 80/20 chuck ground fresh at the meat counter. Ask for a coarse grind, ground today.
Better still: Ask the butcher to grind chuck with brisket or short rib (about 70/30).
Avoid: Pre-packaged tubes and anything leaner than 80/20.

  • Keep it cold until it hits the griddle. Cold fat renders into crust.
  • Handle as little as possible.
  • No salt in the meat. Salt goes on the surface after smashing.

Steps

  1. Fry sauce. Whisk everything. Chill 15 minutes.
  2. Portion. Weigh into 8 balls of 3 oz each. Roll loosely. Back in the fridge.
  3. Toast buns. Butter the cut sides. Toast on the griddle over medium until golden.
  4. Heat the griddle hard. High heat, 5–10 minutes, until a drop of water vanishes instantly. Wipe a very thin film of oil across it.
  5. Smash. 2–4 balls with space between. Parchment on top, and within 30 seconds press hard until about ¼ inch thick. Hold 10 seconds. Peel the parchment. Smash once only.
  6. Season. Salt and pepper the tops right away.
  7. Sear. 2 minutes, untouched, until the edges are lacy and deep brown.
  8. Scrape and flip. Sharp edge under the patty, scrape hard — the crust belongs on the burger. Flip.
  9. Cheese and stack. Cheese on each. After 20 seconds, stack one patty on the other. 20–30 seconds more.
  10. Build. Bottom bun → fry sauce → pickles → onion → double stack → top bun with more fry sauce. Eat now.

Pair With

  • Fries or tots with extra fry sauce
  • Jalapeño Crema Slaw
  • Red, Black & White Bean Salad

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. On the surface after smashing — seasons the crust without changing the texture inside.

Fat. 80/20 minimum. The patty fries in its own rendering fat, which builds the crust.

Acid. Pickles and pickle brine cut the beef fat.

Sweet. Ketchup, the soft potato bun, raw onion.

Umami. The Maillard crust is the point. Smashing maximizes contact, so a thin patty is almost all crust.

Heat (temperature). A thin patty cooks through in under 3 minutes, so the griddle has to brown it in that time.

Variants

  • Oklahoma onion burger: Pile paper-thin onion on the ball and smash through it.
  • Charcoal-heat griddle: Cast iron directly over a full chimney of coals. Hotter than a stovetop, smoke stays outside.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Gray, no crust Griddle not hot enough
Patty sticks to spatula Use parchment
Crust left on griddle Scrape harder with a sharp-edged spatula
Uneven patties Weigh the balls
Dry burger Too lean, or smashed twice

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Salt ground meat at the last minute. Salt mixed in early makes it springy like sausage.
  • Heat creates crust. Thin food needs very high heat to brown before it overcooks.

Notes for the Kids

  • Ask the butcher to grind it fresh. Best burger upgrade there is.
  • Use the scale. 3 oz every time.
  • Hot griddle, smash once, don't touch it.
  • Salt after smashing, never in the meat.

Fat Burgers

Beef and sausage, herbed, cheese mixed in — charcoal-grilled

Makes7–9 patties (6–8 oz each)
Active30 min
Freeze2 hrs
Grill~15 min
EquipmentLarge bowl, food-safe gloves, kitchen scale, sheet pan, wax paper, charcoal grill, instant-read thermometer

Ingredients

Meat

  • 2 lb ground beef, 80/20, cold
  • 1 lb uncased pork sausage, cold — any bold, savory sausage. Not breakfast or sweet sausage.

Binder

  • 2 large eggs
  • ¾ cup panko

Herbs (go heavy)

  • 2 Tbsp fresh thyme leaves, chopped
  • 2 Tbsp fresh oregano leaves, chopped

Seasoning

  • 1 tsp garlic salt
  • 2 tsp paprika (sweet or smoked)
  • 1 tsp coarse black pepper
  • 1 Tbsp Worcestershire
  • 1 Tbsp soy sauce
  • Kosher salt: ¼ tsp only

Cheese

  • 1 cup (4 oz) shredded cheese, to taste. Go strong — extra-sharp cheddar, pepper jack, smoked gouda, or Gruyère.

Build

  • Brioche or potato buns, toasted
  • Lettuce, tomato, onion, pickles, sauces of choice

Salt note

The sausage, garlic salt, soy, Worcestershire, and cheese already land right around the book's 1% rule. Add only the ¼ tsp kosher salt, then cook a test patty.

Steps

Mix and form

  1. Panade. Whisk eggs, Worcestershire, and soy. Stir in panko. Soak 5 minutes into a paste.
  2. Season. Add herbs, garlic salt, paprika, pepper, and salt to the paste.
  3. Mix by hand. Gloves on. Add beef, sausage, and cheese. Mix with fingertips just until combined, about 1 minute.
  4. Test patty. Fry a tablespoon-size bite. Taste. Adjust now.
  5. Weigh and form. 6–8 oz portions, about 1 inch thick and ½ inch wider than the bun.
  6. Make the donut. Press your thumb through the center for a 1-inch hole. It keeps the patty flat and closes up on the grill.
  7. Freeze to firm. On wax paper, not touching, 2 hours. To store: freeze fully, stack with wax paper in a freezer bag, up to 3 months.

Grill

  1. Two-zone fire. Direct side ~450–500°F.
  2. Sear direct. Straight from the freezer. 4–5 minutes, lid open, without moving.
  3. Flip and sear 4 minutes more.
  4. Finish indirect. Cool side, lid on, until the center reads 160°F, about 4–8 minutes.
  5. Toast buns on the direct side while patties finish.
  6. Rest 5 minutes.

Pair With

  • Grilled corn on the cob
  • Hot-Pan Blistered Green Beans
  • Chilled Lemon Asparagus
  • Pickled Beet & Burrata Salad

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Spread across sausage, garlic salt, soy, Worcestershire, and cheese — which is why the test patty matters more than any measurement.

Fat. Beef fat plus pork fat plus a strong cheese that melts into pockets.

Acid. Worcestershire adds a quiet tang. Pickles and tomato do the rest.

Sweet. Paprika, the browned crust, and Worcestershire's molasses.

Umami. Soy and Worcestershire layered on beef, pork, aged cheese, and a charcoal crust. The most umami-dense recipe in the book.

Heat (temperature). Direct heat for crust, indirect to cook the thick center. Freezing buys more time on direct heat.

Variants

  • Hot: Hot Italian sausage plus ½ tsp red pepper flakes.
  • Stuffed: Two thin 4 oz patties with a cube of cheese sealed between.
  • Smoky: A chunk of hickory on the coals.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Patties fall apart Not frozen long enough
Sticking to grates Grates not clean, oiled, or hot
Outside burnt, inside raw Move to indirect sooner
Domed burgers Donut hole too small or skipped
Dense/rubbery Overmixed
Too salty Skip the kosher salt entirely

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Fat carries flavor. Aromatic compounds dissolve in fat, so fat spreads the herbs and garlic through every bite.
  • Taste raw mixtures by cooking a bite.

Notes for the Kids

  • Gloves on. Cold meat, quick mixing.
  • Fry a test patty before you make 8 burgers.
  • The thumb hole isn't a mistake.
  • Pork and raw egg: always cook to 160°F.
  • Make a double batch and freeze half.

Baked Fresh Cod, Three Ways

On a bed of shallots or onions, cooked to temperature with a probe thermometer

Serves4
Active10 min
Total25–30 min
Equipment9×13 baking dish or rimmed sheet pan, probe thermometer (CHEF iQ or similar), paper towels

The Base Method (all three)

Why the bed

Fish releases liquid as it bakes. Laid straight on the pan, the bottom steams soft and gray. A bed of thin-sliced shallots or onions lifts the fillets out of it, catches the drippings, and turns into a soft, sweet side you spoon over the fish.

Why the probe

Cod goes from silky to dry in a couple of minutes.

  • Target: pull at 140°F. Carryover brings it to about 145°F while it rests.
  • Insert the probe horizontally from the side into the center of the thickest fillet.
  • In the CHEF iQ app, set a custom target of 140°F. It alerts you when it's time.

Base ingredients

  • 4 fresh cod fillets, 1–1½ inches thick (~1½ lb total). Thick, center-cut fillets bake evenly; thin tail pieces don't.
  • 2 Tbsp olive oil or melted butter
  • The bed: ~2 large shallots or 1 medium onion, sliced thin
  • 1–2 lemons

Base steps

  1. Oven to 400°F. Rack in the middle.
  2. Dry the fish. Pat fillets completely dry.
  3. Build the bed. Toss the shallots or onions with half the fat and a pinch of salt. Spread where the fillets will sit. Add lemon slices on top if the option calls for it.
  4. Season the fish. Brush with the remaining fat. Season per your option. Let sit 10 minutes.
  5. Arrange. Fillets on the bed, not touching. Tuck thin ends under.
  6. Probe. Thickest fillet. Target 140°F.
  7. Bake about 12–18 minutes, until the probe hits 140°F.
  8. Optional color: Broil 1–2 minutes. Watch it.
  9. Rest 3 minutes. Finish per your option. Serve with the softened shallots spooned over.

Option A — Kinder's Lemon Butter Garlic

Rich, buttery, bright — the classic

  • Bed: 2 large shallots + 4 thin lemon slices
  • Fat: 2 Tbsp melted butter
  • Seasoning: 2–3 tsp Kinder's Lemon Butter Garlic (no extra salt)
  • Fresh: 2 garlic cloves, thin-sliced, over the shallots
  • Finish: Squeeze of lemon, 1 Tbsp chopped fresh dill

Option B — Kinder's Lemon & Herb

Herbal, French-style, lighter

  • Bed: 2 large shallots + 4 thin lemon slices
  • Fat: 2 Tbsp olive oil
  • Seasoning: 2–3 tsp Kinder's Lemon & Herb (no extra salt)
  • Fresh: 1 garlic clove, grated into the oil
  • Finish: Lemon, fresh dill and a little parsley; a few tarragon leaves if you have them

Option C — Homemade Lemon-Dill

Clean, bright, classic

  • 1½ tsp Morton kosher salt — 1% of fish weight
  • ½ tsp black pepper
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 1 Tbsp each fresh dill and parsley, chopped
  • 1 garlic clove, grated
  • Bed: 2 large shallots + 4 thin lemon slices
  • Fat: 2 Tbsp butter — 1 melted for brushing, 1 in small pats on top
  • Method: Brush, press the blend onto the tops, dot with butter.
  • Finish: Juice of ½ lemon and more fresh dill.

Other Kinder's blends

Lemon Pepper Sugar is the first ingredient — can taste sweet on mild cod and burns under the broiler
Blackened Made for screaming-hot cast iron, not a 400°F oven
Buttery Garlic & Herb Works, but salty with no lemon. Add fresh lemon
Roasted Garlic Brown Butter Better on heartier fish like grouper or halibut
Chipotle Pineapple Overpowers cod. Use it for fish tacos

Pair With

  • Chilled Lemon Asparagus
  • Hot-Pan Blistered Green Beans
  • Chilled Lentils with Shallot Vinaigrette
  • Plain rice
  • Pickled Beet & Burrata Salad

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Cod is lean and mild, so it needs real seasoning — but only 10 minutes ahead; longer starts to cure the flesh. Kinder's blends carry their own salt.

Fat. Lean fish needs added fat to taste rich and stay moist. The bed catches the fat and juices.

Acid. Citrus twice: slices under the fish infuse gently; a fresh squeeze at the end brightens.

Sweet. Shallots soften and sweeten in the oven.

Umami. Garlic, butter, and the savory shallot-bed juices.

Heat (temperature). 400°F cooks thick fillets fast enough to stay moist. The probe ends the cook at the right moment.

Variants

  • Other fish: Haddock, halibut, grouper, snapper — same 140°F pull. Salmon: pull at 125°F for medium.
  • Foil packets: Same layers in foil, on the grill over medium indirect heat.
  • Add vegetables: Cherry tomatoes and asparagus around the fillets (asparagus for the last 8 minutes).
  • Capers: 1 Tbsp over Option A or C.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Fish dry Pull at 140°F; carryover does the rest
Bottom mushy Bed too thin. Full layer under each fillet
Probe reading jumps Tip touching the pan. Reinsert from the side, centered
Uneven cooking Tuck thin ends under
Too salty Added salt with a Kinder's blend. Skip it

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Delicate food wants gentle handling. Pull lean fish early and let carryover finish.
  • Salt fish shortly before cooking. Ten to fifteen minutes is plenty.

Notes for the Kids

  • Thick fillets. Thin ones overcook before you can blink.
  • Dry the fish. Every time.
  • The onion bed keeps the bottom from going soggy, and the onions are the best part.
  • Trust the probe. 140°F, then rest.
  • Fresh cod smells like clean ocean, not fishy.

Butter-Basted Seared Scallops

Dry scallops, a smoking-hot cast iron pan, and a quick lemon-shallot pan sauce

Serves3–4 as a main, 6 as a starter
Active25 min
Total40 min
Equipment12-inch cast iron, paper towels, instant-read thermometer, tongs, spoon for basting
Inspired byAmerica's Test Kitchen's pan-seared scallops, reworked with the book's salt rule and a pan sauce

Buy the Right Scallops

  • Dry scallops have no additives. Off-white or pinkish, a little tacky, and they sear to a deep crust. Ask for "dry-packed" and look for U-10 or U-12 (under 10–12 per pound).
  • Wet scallops are soaked in a phosphate solution. Bright white, sitting in milky liquid. They steam instead of sear.
  • The microwave test: One scallop on a paper towel, 15 seconds. Dry leaves a small damp patch; wet leaves a wide wet ring.
  • If all you can find is wet: Soak 30 minutes in 1 qt cold water + ¼ cup lemon juice + 2 Tbsp kosher salt. Then dry as below and use only pepper.

Ingredients

Scallops

  • 1½ lb large dry sea scallops (12–16)
  • 1 tsp Morton kosher salt, divided — about 1% of weight (dry scallops only)
  • ½ tsp coarse black pepper

To cook

  • 2 Tbsp avocado oil, divided
  • 2 Tbsp unsalted butter, divided

Lemon-shallot pan sauce

  • 1 large shallot, finely minced
  • 1 Tbsp fig vinegar or white wine
  • 3 Tbsp cold unsalted butter, cubed
  • 1 Tbsp lemon juice, plus zest of ½ lemon
  • 1 Tbsp chopped parsley or chives
  • Pinch of flake salt

Steps

1. Prep

  1. Remove the side muscle. Peel off the small, tough rectangle on the side of each scallop.
  2. Dry hard. Between layers of paper towel, press gently. Sit 10 minutes. Pat dry again. Dry scallops brown; wet scallops steam.
  3. Season at the last minute. Salt and pepper both sides right before the pan.

2. Sear

  1. Heat the pan. Cast iron over high heat 5 minutes. 1 Tbsp oil; it should shimmer and just start to smoke.
  2. First batch. Half the scallops, flat side down, space between. Don't touch them for 1½–2 minutes until deep golden and they release on their own.
  3. Flip. Add 1 Tbsp butter. It will foam.
  4. Baste. Tilt the pan and spoon foaming butter over for 30–90 seconds, until the centers read 115°F. They rise to about 120–125°F as they rest.
  5. Rest and repeat. Warm plate, uncovered. Wipe the pan. Repeat.

3. Pan sauce (60 seconds)

  1. Heat to medium-low. Pour off all but 1 tsp fat.
  2. Shallot, 30 seconds.
  3. Fig vinegar or wine; scrape up the brown bits. Reduce to about a tablespoon.
  4. Off the heat, swirl in cold butter a cube at a time until glossy. Lemon zest and juice, parsley, pinch of salt.
  5. Spoon around the scallops so the crust stays crisp. Serve now.

Pair With

  • Chilled Lemon Asparagus
  • Seared Brussels Sprouts with Fish Sauce Vinaigrette
  • Buttered pasta tossed with the pan sauce
  • Pickled Beet & Burrata Salad

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Scallops are mostly water, so salt goes on at the last second — early salt pulls water and ruins the sear.

Fat. Two fats, two jobs: oil for a hot pan, butter after the flip so it foams and flavors instead of burning.

Acid. Lemon and fig vinegar at the end keep sweet, rich scallops from tasting flat.

Sweet. Scallops are the sweetest seafood. The sear caramelizes their natural sugars.

Umami. Glutamate-rich scallops, a browned crust, and fond in the sauce.

Heat (temperature). Ripping-hot pan, dry surface, no crowding. Pull at 115°F; 125°F is already heading to rubbery.

Variants

  • Charcoal grill: Two parallel skewers, oil, high direct heat, 90 seconds per side.
  • Brown butter and sage: Skip the shallot. Brown the butter, crisp 6 sage leaves, finish with lemon.
  • Over pasta: On buttered linguine with the pan sauce.
  • Bay scallops: One very hot batch, 60 seconds per side.
  • No fig vinegar: Dry white wine or white wine vinegar.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Pale, no crust Wet scallops, pan not hot, or crowded
Stick to the pan Not ready to flip. Wait 15–20 seconds
Rubbery Pull at 115°F, not 125°F
Milky liquid in the pan Wet scallops. Use the rescue soak
Butter burned Added before the flip, or heat too high
Sauce broke Pan too hot. Off heat, cold butter, one cube at a time
Tough strip on the side Side muscle wasn't removed

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Dryness is half the sear.
  • Use two fats. Oil for heat, butter for flavor.
  • Pull early. Delicate food finishes cooking off the heat.
  • Enrich a sauce with cold butter, off the heat.

Notes for the Kids

  • Ask for "dry" scallops. If they're white and sitting in milk, they're wet.
  • Peel off the little side muscle.
  • Dry them. Wait 10 minutes. Dry them again.
  • Don't move them. They let go when they're ready.
  • Two batches, not one crowded pan.

Draft notes

  • Pan sauce is an addition to the source recipe — keep it, or go lemon wedge only.
  • Use an instant-read thermometer; scallops are too small to hold the CHEF iQ probe.
  • Test the wet-scallop rescue soak once with your own seafood counter.

Lentils

  • 1 cup French green (du Puy) or black beluga lentils, rinsed
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 garlic clove, smashed

Vinaigrette

  • ¼ cup rosemary oil — or extra-virgin olive oil
  • 4 tsp fig vinegar — or sherry or red wine vinegar
  • 2 tsp Dijon
  • 2 shallots, finely minced
  • ½ tsp kosher salt
  • ¼ tsp black pepper

Alternate pairing: Blood orange oil + cinnamon pear vinegar. Great with walnuts and dried cranberries.

Steps

  1. Cook the lentils. Stovetop: gentle simmer 18–25 minutes until tender but holding shape. Pressure cooker: high pressure 7 minutes, quick release.
  2. Shallots marinate in the vinegar, Dijon, salt, and pepper while lentils cook. Whisk in oil just before using.
  3. Drain and dress warm. Discard bay and garlic.
  4. Add-ins after 10 minutes of cooling.
  5. Chill 1 hour. Re-taste before serving.

Add-in ideas (pick 3–4)

Add Amount What it brings
Carrot, finely diced 1 medium Sweet crunch
Celery, finely diced 1 stalk Fresh crunch
Fresh parsley ½ cup Bright, grassy
Fresh mint or dill ¼ cup Cool lift
Toasted walnuts ½ cup Rich crunch (add at serving)
Goat cheese or feta ½ cup Creamy, salty (add at serving)

Sun-dried tomatoes ¼ cup Concentrated umami
Dried cranberries or figs ¼ cup Chewy sweetness
Baby spinach or arugula 2 cups Makes it a full salad

Classic combo: Carrot + celery + parsley + goat cheese.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Lentils are salted in the cooking water so they're seasoned through. Cold dulls salt — always re-taste after chilling.

Fat. 3:1 coats without being greasy. Flavored oils do double duty.

Acid. Beans and lentils are earthy and starchy, so they need more acid than a leafy salad.

Sweet. A touch of honey, fig vinegar, optional dried fruit.

Umami. Lentils, sun-dried tomatoes, feta, and Dijon.

Heat (temperature). Dress warm. Warm starches absorb; cold ones repel.

Variants

  • From dried beans: Pressure cook each separately with salted water: kidney 25 min, black 22 min, white 25 min, natural release.
  • Southwest beans: Corn, jalapeño, and lime in place of half the vinegar.
  • Mediterranean beans: Chickpeas instead of black beans; lemon oil; olives and feta.
  • Grain bowl: Over warm farro or quinoa.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Lentils mushy Boiled too hard. Use green or black, never red
Lentils still hard Old lentils. Keep simmering, 5 min at a time
Bland after chilling Salt and a splash of vinegar before serving
Beans taste "canned" Rinse more thoroughly
Onion too sharp Marinate longer

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Salt beans and lentils while they cook. Salted water seasons them all the way through.
  • Dress starches warm.

Notes for the Kids

  • Rinse canned beans. Always.
  • Dress them while they're warm.
  • Taste it cold before you serve it.
  • Red lentils are for soup, not salad.

Hot-Pan Blistered Green Beans

Kinder's butter seasoning, garlic finish, optional shallots

Serves4
Active15 min
Total15 min (20 with pre-blanch)
Equipment12-inch cast iron, tongs. For pre-blanch: large pot, ice water, towels

Ingredients

  • 1 lb green beans, trimmed
  • 3 Tbsp neutral high-smoke-point oil, added in waves
  • 2 shallots, thin-sliced — optional
  • 1½–2 tsp Kinder's seasoning (salt is already in both — no added salt): Caramelized Onion Butter (sweeter) or Roasted Garlic Brown Butter (nutty, garlicky)
  • 3 garlic cloves, thin-sliced
  • Optional: lemon wedge

Steps

  1. Dry the beans. Completely. Water steams instead of blisters.
  2. Heat hard. Cast iron over high until it just starts to smoke.
  3. Wave 1. 1 Tbsp oil, beans in a single layer. Don't touch for 2–3 minutes.
  4. Wave 2. Toss. When the pan looks dry, drizzle 1 Tbsp oil around the edges. Shallots now if using. 2–3 minutes.
  5. Wave 3. Last 1 Tbsp oil. Seasoning in. Toss 1–2 minutes until crisp-tender.
  6. Garlic last. Drop to medium. Garlic 30–60 seconds until golden. Off heat.
  7. Finish. Taste. Optional lemon. Serve now.

Option: Pre-blanch

  1. Boil well-salted water. Beans in 2 minutes.
  2. Ice water, 2 minutes.
  3. Dry very thoroughly. Up to 2 days ahead, refrigerated.
  4. Hot-pan time drops to 4–5 minutes.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. The Kinder's blend carries the salt. It goes on late because its onion bits and sugar scorch.

Fat. Oil in waves keeps a thin, hot film. All at once fries them greasy; too little scorches.

Acid. Lemon at the end brightens the char.

Sweet. High heat caramelizes the beans' sugars. Shallots add mellow sweetness.

Umami. Charred edges and toasty brown-butter flavor.

Heat (temperature). Hot cast iron blisters the skin before the inside goes soft. Garlic burns in seconds, so it's last, on lower heat.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
No char Wet beans or pan not hot
Seasoning burned Added too early
Garlic bitter Pan too hot. Lower heat first
Greasy Too much oil at once
Still squeaky Pre-blanch, or cover 1 minute with a splash of water

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Aromatics go in by burn time. Garlic burns fastest, so it goes in last.

Notes for the Kids

  • Dry beans blister. Wet beans steam.
  • Oil goes in when the pan looks dry.
  • Garlic is always last.

Chilled Lemon Asparagus

Blanched and chilled, with olive oil, lemon, salt & pepper — optional bacon aioli

Serves4
Active15 min
Total20 min (aioli +15 min)
EquipmentLarge pot, ice water bowl, towels, skillet (for bacon)

Ingredients

Asparagus

  • 1 lb asparagus
  • 4 qt water + 2 Tbsp Morton kosher salt
  • Large bowl of ice water

Dressing

  • 2 Tbsp good extra-virgin olive oil
  • Zest of ½ lemon + 1 Tbsp juice
  • Flake salt, fresh-cracked black pepper

Optional bacon aioli

  • 4 slices bacon
  • 1 large egg yolk, room temperature (pasteurized if serving young kids, elders, or anyone pregnant)
  • 1 small garlic clove, grated
  • 2 tsp lemon juice
  • 1 tsp Dijon
  • ¼ tsp kosher salt
  • ½ cup neutral oil
  • 2 Tbsp reserved bacon fat, cooled but liquid
  • Pinch of smoked paprika, ¼ tsp black pepper

Steps

Asparagus

  1. Trim. Bend one spear — it snaps where the woody part ends. Cut the rest to match.
  2. Boil the salted water.
  3. Blanch by thickness: pencil-thin 1–1½ min · medium 2–2½ min · thick 3–4 min.
  4. Shock in ice water, 3 minutes.
  5. Dry on towels. Refrigerate (up to 1 day ahead).
  6. Dress just before serving: olive oil, zest, lemon juice, flake salt, pepper.

Optional bacon aioli

  1. Cook bacon until crisp. Reserve 2 Tbsp fat; let it cool until just warm. Combine with the neutral oil.
  2. Whisk yolk, garlic, lemon juice, Dijon, and salt in a bowl set on a damp towel.
  3. Whisking constantly, add the oil drop by drop for the first quarter, then a thin stream. (Or immersion blender in a tall cup, 30 seconds.)
  4. Whisk in paprika and pepper. Taste. Thin with water if stiff.
  5. Chop the bacon. Stir half in; save half for the top.

Mayo shortcut: ½ cup mayo + the garlic, 1 tsp lemon juice, Dijon, paprika, pepper, and 1 Tbsp bacon fat. Then step 5.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Salted water seasons the asparagus from the inside. Flake salt adds crunch.

Fat. Good olive oil is a flavor here — use the nice bottle. The aioli brings smoky, rich contrast.

Acid. Lemon goes on last. Acid left sitting on green vegetables turns them olive-brown.

Sweet. Blanching keeps asparagus' natural sweetness intact.

Umami. Bacon — cured, smoked, and browned.

Heat (temperature). Short hard boil, instant ice bath: crisp-tender and bright green.

Variants

  • Warm: Skip the ice bath; dress hot.
  • Grilled: Oil and salt, high direct heat 3–5 minutes.
  • Toppings: Shaved Parmesan, chopped egg, or toasted almonds.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Limp or gray Overcooked. Time by thickness; have ice ready
Turned olive-brown Lemon added too early
Aioli broke Start a fresh yolk and slowly whisk the broken aioli into it
Woody bites Trim more

Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat Tips

  • Acid dulls green. Dress green vegetables at the last moment.
  • Season the water, season the vegetable.

Notes for the Kids

  • Ice bath ready before anything goes in the pot.
  • Thin spears cook fast.
  • Lemon goes on last.

Pickled Beet & Burrata Salad

Tangy pickled beets, creamy burrata, fresh herbs

Serves4
Active25 min
Total2 hrs minimum (best overnight)
Pickled beets keep2 weeks
EquipmentPressure cooker (or oven), quart jar, gloves, small saucepan

Ingredients

Pickled beets

  • 4 medium fresh beets (about 1⅓ lb), scrubbed
  • 1 cup red wine vinegar or apple cider vinegar
  • ½ cup water
  • ¼ cup sugar
  • 1¼ tsp Morton kosher salt
  • Optional: 1 bay leaf, ½ tsp black peppercorns, 2 strips orange peel

Salad

  • 2 balls burrata (8 oz total)
  • 2 cups arugula (optional)
  • ~½ cup fresh herbs, torn: mint, basil, dill, chives
  • ¼ cup pistachios, toasted and chopped — optional

Dressing

  • 3 Tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 Tbsp beet pickling brine
  • Flake salt, black pepper
  • Honey drizzle — optional

Steps

Cook the beets

  • Pressure cooker: Trivet + 1 cup water. High pressure 18 min (small), 22 (medium), 28 (large). Natural release 10 minutes.
  • Oven: Foil-wrapped, 400°F, 45–60 minutes.

Cool, then rub the skins off with a paper towel (gloves on).

Pickle

  1. Wedges or ½-inch slices into a quart jar.
  2. Boil vinegar, water, sugar, salt, and aromatics.
  3. Pour hot over the beets. Cool, lid, refrigerate. 2 hours minimum; overnight is best.

Build

  1. Burrata out 30 minutes early. Cold burrata tastes like nothing.
  2. Whisk olive oil and brine (3:1).
  3. Arugula, then beets. Tear the burrata open among the beets.
  4. Dressing, herbs, pistachios, flake salt, pepper, optional honey.

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Salt in the brine seasons the beets through. Flake salt wakes up the burrata.

Fat. Burrata's cream is the rich counterpoint to sharp beets.

Acid. The brine doubles as the vinegar in the dressing — everything tastes connected.

Sweet. Beets are naturally sweet; a little sugar balances the vinegar.

Umami. Toasted pistachios add savory depth.

Variants

  • Pantry dressing: Blood orange oil + fig vinegar (3:1).
  • Golden beets: Pickle separately so the red doesn't bleed.
  • Cheese swap: Fresh mozzarella, goat cheese, or whipped feta.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Beets too sharp Pickled too briefly. More sugar next time
Burrata bland Too cold. Room temp, more flake salt
Herbs wilted Add right before serving

Notes for the Kids

  • Fresh beets only. Canned beets taste like the can.
  • Wear gloves — beets stain.
  • Never throw out the pickling brine. It's your vinegar.

Seared Brussels Sprouts with Fish Sauce Vinaigrette

Cast-iron seared, oven-finished, tossed in a sweet-salty-sour-spicy dressing with mint & cilantro

Serves4–6
Active25 min
Total45–55 min
Dressing keeps1 week
Equipment12-inch cast iron, sheet pan, tongs, small jar
Inspired byMomofuku's roasted Brussels sprouts with fish sauce vinaigrette, rebuilt around the book's pillars

Ingredients

Vinaigrette

  • 2 Tbsp fish sauce
  • 2 Tbsp water
  • 2 Tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 Tbsp lime juice
  • 2 tsp sugar
  • 1 garlic clove, finely grated
  • 1–2 Thai chiles, thin-sliced (or ½ tsp red pepper flakes)

Sprouts

  • 1½ lb Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved through the stem
  • 3 Tbsp avocado oil, divided
  • No added salt — fish sauce is the salt

To finish

  • ¼ cup fresh mint, torn
  • ¼ cup cilantro, chopped
  • Optional: crushed roasted peanuts or crispy shallots; lime wedge

Steps

  1. Dressing. Shake everything until the sugar dissolves. Taste on a raw sprout leaf: very salty, very sour, faintly sweet.
  2. Prep. Sprouts completely dry. Halve through the stem. Oven to 400°F.
  3. Sear. Cast iron over medium-high, 1½ Tbsp oil. Sprouts cut side down, tight single layer. Don't touch for 4–5 minutes until deep, dark brown.
  4. Oven. Slide the skillet in. 10–15 minutes until a knife slips into the stem end.
  5. Dress hot. Toss with ¾ of the dressing, then herbs. Taste, add more dressing or lime. Top with peanuts or shallots.

Pair With

  • Butter-Basted Seared Scallops
  • Smoked Pork Ribs or Grilled Chicken Thighs
  • Ground Beef Bulgogi Rice Bowls

Why This Works (Five Pillars)

Salt. Fish sauce is liquid salt with extra flavor, added after cooking so it doesn't pull water early.

Fat. A hot film of oil browns the cut face.

Acid. Rice vinegar and lime cut the char and stay bright because they go on after.

Sweet. Searing caramelizes the sprouts' sugars; a little sugar in the dressing tames bitterness.

Umami. Fish sauce is fermented anchovy, packed with glutamate.

Heat (temperature). Sear for color, oven to cook the center.

Variants

  • Sheet pan for a crowd: 450°F, cut side down on a preheated pan, 20–25 minutes.
  • Vegetarian: 2 Tbsp soy sauce + 1 tsp miso instead of fish sauce.
  • Other vegetables: Great on charred broccoli, cabbage, or green beans.

Troubleshooting

Problem Fix
Pale Pan not hot, sprouts wet, or crowded
Burnt outside, hard center Give the oven the full time
Too salty Thin dressing with water and lime
Too fishy More lime and a pinch of sugar

Notes for the Kids

  • Sear them until they're almost burnt.
  • Dry them. Cut side down. Don't touch them.
  • The dressing smells strong. Trust it.
  • Taste the sauce before you pour it all on.

Draft notes

  • Dressing amounts are a starting point; test once and adjust.
  • Red Boat or Three Crabs fish sauce taste cleaner than most supermarket brands.

Ninja Creami High-Protein Base

The tested 24 oz ratio every flavor starts from

Makes1 pint (24 oz Creami tub)
Active5 min
Freeze24 hrs
Equipment24 oz Ninja Creami, whisk or blender bottle

Ingredients

  • 1 Premier Protein shake (11 oz), any flavor
  • 2 Tbsp sugar-free Jell-O instant pudding mix
  • ½ tsp guar gum
  • 2 Tbsp monk fruit sweetener
  • For the re-spin: 1 Tbsp heavy whipping cream

Steps

  1. Dry first. Whisk pudding mix, guar gum, and sweetener together in a small bowl. Guar clumps if it hits liquid alone.
  2. Wet in. Pour the shake into the tub. Whisk in the dry mix a little at a time until completely smooth.
  3. Freeze level. Lid on, flat, 24 hours.
  4. Spin on Lite Ice Cream — not the regular Ice Cream program. High-protein bases go gummy on full Ice Cream.
  5. Re-spin. Crumbly or powdery is normal for a casein base. Add 1 Tbsp heavy cream and re-spin.
  6. Mix-ins (optional) last, with the Mix-In program.

Flavors in the Book

  • Peanut Butter Protein — base plus PB powder.
  • Orange Lemon — bright citrus version.

Why This Works

Fat. A protein shake is lean. A tablespoon of cream at the re-spin adds the fat that makes it feel like ice cream.

Sweet. Monk fruit sweetens without adding sugar; pudding mix adds body and flavor.

Texture. The Creami shaves a frozen block instead of churning it. Pudding mix and guar keep ice crystals small so the shave turns creamy, not icy.

Rules

  • Lite Ice Cream program for protein bases.
  • Expect one re-spin.
  • No banana. Ever.
