# Baked Potato, Oiled and Salted
### Dry skin, bare rack, fluffy middle. Plus a topping bar and chili garlic crunch oil

**Serves:** 4 | **Active:** 10 min | **Total:** about 1 hr 15 min
**Equipment:** Oven (or pellet smoker / charcoal grill with a lid), sheet pan, CHEF iQ probe, fork

## Ingredients

- 4 large russet potatoes (about 10–12 oz each)
- 2 Tbsp neutral oil or rosemary oil
- 1½ tsp coarse kosher salt (about 1% of the potatoes' weight is the target; use more on the skin if you like it salty)
- Butter and flaky salt to finish

## Steps

1. **Heat the oven to 425°F** with a rack in the middle. Put a sheet pan on the rack below to catch drips.
2. **Scrub and dry completely.** Scrub under running water, then dry with a towel until the skin feels dry to the touch. Wet skin steams and turns leathery.
3. **Poke 4–5 times** with a fork so steam can leave.
4. **Oil and salt.** Rub each potato with about 1½ tsp oil, then roll in coarse salt. The oil helps the salt stick and crisps the skin.
5. **Bake directly on the rack, no foil.** Foil traps steam and gives you a soft, boiled skin. Bake 50–60 minutes.
6. **Check with the probe.** The center should read **205–210°F**. At that temperature the starch is fully gelatinized and the inside turns fluffy. A fork should slide in with no resistance.
7. **Split right away.** Slice open the top and squeeze the ends toward the middle to break up the inside. Trapped steam makes the inside gummy as it cools.
8. **Add butter and a pinch of flaky salt** while it's hot, then go to the topping bar.

### Option: Smoker or charcoal grill
Cook at 400–425°F, indirect heat, same internal temp. Add a small chunk of wood or run competition-blend pellets for a light smoke. Skin gets a little chewier and the flavor is much bigger.

### Option: Make ahead
Bake, cool, refrigerate whole. Reheat at 425°F for 15–20 minutes so the skin crisps again. Don't microwave, it softens the skin.

## Chili Garlic Crunch Oil (optional topper)

Makes about 1 cup. Keeps 2 weeks in the fridge.

- ½ cup neutral oil
- 6 garlic cloves, thin-sliced
- 2 Tbsp crushed red pepper flakes or Korean gochugaru
- 2 Tbsp sesame seeds
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp sugar
- 1 Tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp rice vinegar (or hot honey vinegar)

1. Put the garlic and cold oil in a small pot. Heat over medium until the garlic is pale gold, 4–5 minutes. Lower the heat if it browns fast.
2. Pour the oil and garlic through a strainer into a heat-proof bowl. Set the crisp garlic on a towel.
3. Put the pepper flakes, sesame seeds, salt and sugar in a bowl and pour the hot oil over them. It should sizzle.
4. Let it cool 2–3 minutes (soy sauce spatters in hot oil), then stir in the soy sauce and vinegar. Add the crisp garlic back at the end so it stays crunchy.

## Topping Bar (Regular Potato)

Set out in bowls and let everyone build their own.

- **Rich and creamy:** butter, sour cream, Chipotle-Lime Crema (in the Sauces chapter), shredded sharp cheddar
- **Smoky and meaty:** crisp bacon, chopped smoked pork butt or beef from the smoker, pressure cooker chili
- **Fresh and sharp:** chives or sliced scallions, Quick-Pickled Red Onions (Sides), pickled jalapeños
- **Crunch:** chili garlic crunch oil, crushed fried onions
- **Heat:** Charred Pineapple-Habanero Hot Sauce (Sauces chapter), hot sauce of your choice

**Easy combos:** Butter + sour cream + chives + bacon. Smoked pork + Carolina Mustard Sauce + pickled onions. Cheddar + chili + scallions.

## Why This Works (Five Pillars)

**Salt.** Coarse salt on the skin seasons the part you actually taste first, and flaky salt at the end adds crunch. The inside is mostly plain starch, so toppings should be seasoned boldly.

**Fat.** A thin film of oil conducts heat and crisps the skin. Butter and crema inside carry flavor into the starch.

**Acid.** Pickled onions, vinegar in the crunch oil, and lime crema cut through the richness. A baked potato is heavy on starch and fat and needs the brightness.

**Sweet.** A little sugar in the crunch oil rounds out the heat and bitterness. Smoke and char add their own sweetness.

**Umami.** Soy sauce, bacon, smoked meat and aged cheddar give savory depth.

**Heat (temperature).** High oven heat dries the skin while the inside steams to 205–210°F. Lower than that, you get a dense, waxy center.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Soft, wet skin | Skin wasn't dry, foil was used, or the pan below wasn't removing steam. Dry fully and bake on the bare rack |
| Dense, waxy middle | Pulled too early. Cook to 205–210°F and split right away |
| Salt falls off | Oil first, then roll in salt while the oil is wet |
| Burnt garlic in crunch oil | Started in hot oil. Start cold and strain at pale gold; the oil's heat keeps cooking it |
| Gummy inside | Waited to split. Open it and fluff it as soon as it comes out |

# Baked Sweet Potato, Oiled and Salted
### Caramelized skin, creamy middle. Plus a topping bar with cottage cheese and balsamic

**Serves:** 4 | **Active:** 10 min | **Total:** about 1 hr 15 min
**Equipment:** Oven (or pellet smoker / charcoal grill with a lid), sheet pan lined with foil, CHEF iQ probe, fork

## Ingredients

- 4 medium sweet potatoes (about 8–10 oz each), similar in size
- 2 Tbsp neutral oil
- 1 tsp coarse kosher salt (plus flaky salt to finish)

## Steps

1. **Heat the oven to 425°F.** Line a sheet pan with foil and set it on the rack below. Sweet potato sugars leak out and burn onto the pan, and foil makes cleanup easy.
2. **Scrub and dry completely.** Dry skin crisps; wet skin steams.
3. **Poke 4–5 times** with a fork.
4. **Oil and salt.** Rub each with about ½ tsp oil, then roll in coarse salt.
5. **Bake on the bare rack,** 45–60 minutes depending on size. Flip once about halfway if the bottoms are browning faster.
6. **Check with the probe.** Look for **205°F** in the thickest part. The sugars will have leaked and caramelized on the skin, and a fork slides in with no resistance. The skin should look slightly shrunken and wrinkled.
7. **Rest 5 minutes,** then split lengthwise and press the ends in to open it up. Sweet potato stays creamy, so there's no need to fluff hard.

### Option: Smoker or charcoal grill
Cook at 400–425°F, indirect heat, to 205°F. Smoke and sweet potato are an old pairing; a mild wood works well.

### Option: Make ahead
Bake, cool, refrigerate. Reheat at 400°F for 15 minutes. Good for lunch bowls and meal prep.

## Topping Bar (Sweet Potato)

Set out in bowls and let everyone build their own.

- **The signature combo:** cottage cheese + a drizzle of balsamic vinegar + flaky salt + black pepper. The cottage cheese gives cool, salty, high-protein creaminess; the balsamic adds acid and a syrupy sweet-tart note against the sweet potato.
- **Creamy:** cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, Chipotle-Lime Crema, butter
- **Acid:** balsamic vinegar, fig vinegar, cinnamon pear vinegar, hot honey vinegar, lime wedges
- **Smoky and savory:** crisp bacon, pulled pork or beef from the smoker, black beans, pressure cooker carnitas
- **Crunch:** toasted pecans or pepitas, crushed peanuts, crispy fried onions
- **Fresh:** cilantro, scallions, pickled red onions, Avocado-Tomatillo Salsa Verde
- **Heat:** hot sauce, jalapeño, Charred Pineapple-Habanero Hot Sauce
- **Sweet (breakfast style):** butter, cinnamon, maple, toasted walnuts, Greek yogurt

**Easy combos:** Cottage cheese + balsamic + black pepper + toasted pecans. Black beans + chipotle crema + cilantro + pickled onions. Pulled pork + Carolina Mustard Sauce + scallions.

## Why This Works (Five Pillars)

**Salt.** Sweet potato is sweet before anything else, and salt makes that sweetness taste fuller instead of flat. Cottage cheese adds more salt right where it's needed.

**Fat.** Oil crisps the skin; butter or crema carry the flavor into the flesh and round off the sweetness.

**Acid.** Balsamic is the star. Sweet potato is a lot of sugar with little contrast. Acid brings balance and keeps the dish from tasting heavy.

**Sweet.** Slow, steady heat converts starch into sugars and caramelizes whatever leaks out. That's why it's sweeter baked than boiled.

**Umami.** Cottage cheese, bacon and smoked meat add savory weight to balance the sugar.

**Heat (temperature).** A longer, slightly lower bake than regular potatoes gives enzymes in the potato time to convert starch to sugar before the interior is fully cooked.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Burnt syrupy mess on the pan | Sugar leaked onto bare metal. Foil-line the pan below the rack |
| Stringy, firm center | Not cooked enough. Cook to 205°F and test with a fork |
| Tastes flat or cloying | Needs acid and salt. Add balsamic, vinegar or lime and a pinch of flaky salt |
| Skin soggy | Skin wasn't dry, or foil wrapped around the potato. Bake bare |
| Uneven doneness | Mixed sizes in one batch. Pick similar potatoes or pull smaller ones early |

# Barbacoa (Chipotle Copycat)
### Chuck roast braised in a chipotle, cider vinegar and clove adobo, then shredded. Slow cooker, pressure cooker or smoker

**Serves:** 8 (about 12 tacos or 6 burrito bowls) | **Active:** 30 min | **Total:** 4–10 hr depending on method, plus optional overnight salt
**Equipment:** Blender, cast iron or Dutch oven for searing, plus one of: slow cooker, pressure cooker, or pellet smoker; CHEF iQ probe

## Ingredients

**Beef**
- 3 lb beef chuck roast, cut into 3–4 large chunks, fat cap trimmed to about ¼ inch
- 2 tsp Morton kosher salt
- 1 Tbsp neutral oil

**Adobo**
- ⅓ cup apple cider vinegar
- 2 Tbsp fresh lime juice (1 lime)
- 2–4 chipotle peppers in adobo, plus 1 Tbsp of the adobo sauce (2 = mild, 4 = Chipotle-level kick)
- 6 garlic cloves
- 1 Tbsp ground cumin
- 2 tsp dried Mexican oregano (regular oregano works)
- 1 tsp black pepper
- ¼–½ tsp ground cloves (start with ¼; clove gets loud fast)
- ½ tsp Morton kosher salt
- 1 tsp fish sauce (optional; nobody will taste it, but the beef will taste beefier)

**Braise and finish**
- ¾ cup beef broth (low-sodium)
- 3 bay leaves
- 1 more lime, for finishing

## Steps

1. **Salt the beef (overnight if you can).** Pat the chunks dry. Season all over with 2 tsp salt. Refrigerate uncovered on a rack, 1 hour minimum, overnight best.
2. **Blend the adobo.** Put everything in the Adobo list into a blender and blend until completely smooth.
3. **Sear.** Pat the beef dry again. Heat the oil in cast iron over medium-high until it shimmers. Sear the chunks hard on two or three sides, 3–4 minutes per side. Don't crowd the pan; work in batches. You want deep brown, not gray.
4. **Braise** using one of the methods below. In each, the beef sits in the adobo, broth and bay leaves.
5. **Shred.** Pull out the beef, discard the bay leaves, and shred with two forks. Skim the fat off the liquid.
6. **Reduce and toss.** Simmer the braising liquid in a pan until it's reduced by about a third and tastes bold. Ladle about 1 cup back over the shredded beef and toss. Save the rest for reheating.
7. **Finish.** Squeeze the second lime over the meat, toss, and taste. Add salt by the pinch if it tastes flat.

### Method 1: Slow cooker (closest to the restaurant)
Beef, adobo, broth and bay leaves into the slow cooker. **Low 8–10 hours** (or high 5–6) until it shreds with no resistance.

### Method 2: Pressure cooker (weeknight)
Sear right in the pot on sauté, then deglaze with the broth, scraping up the brown bits (this prevents a burn warning). Add the adobo and bay leaves. **High pressure 60 minutes, natural release 15 minutes.** Reduce the liquid on sauté in step 6.

### Method 3: Smoker, then braise (best flavor)
Skip the sear. Smoke the salted chunks at **250°F** with competition-blend pellets until they hit about **165°F** inside and have good bark, 2½–3 hours. Move to a foil pan, pour the adobo, broth and bay leaves over, cover tight with foil, and keep cooking at **300°F** until the probe slides in like butter, about **205°F**, 2–3 more hours.

### Option: Crispy edges
Spread the sauced meat in hot cast iron and leave it alone 2–3 minutes until the bottom crisps. Great for tacos.

## Serve With

Warm corn or flour tortillas, cilantro-lime rice, black beans. From the book: Quick-Pickled Red Onions, Avocado-Tomatillo Salsa Verde, Chipotle-Lime Crema, Jalapeño Crema Slaw. Also good on a baked potato or sweet potato, or in quesadillas.

## Why This Works (Five Pillars)

**Salt.** Salting ahead seasons the beef all the way through and helps it hold moisture during the long cook. The adobo and broth carry salt too, so the sauce gets only a little.

**Fat.** Chuck has plenty of fat and collagen, which melts into gelatin and makes the meat silky. Skim the loose fat so the sauce clings instead of going greasy.

**Acid.** Acid twice: cider vinegar in the braise sets the tangy backbone, and fresh lime at the end wakes it back up after hours of cooking dull it.

**Sweet.** No sugar added. Sweetness comes from browning, smoke, and the chipotles themselves (smoked, dried jalapeños). If it tastes harsh, a teaspoon of honey in the reduced sauce rounds it out.

**Umami.** A hard sear and the optional fish sauce stack savory depth. Reducing the liquid concentrates it.

**Heat (temperature).** Collagen breaks down between about 160°F and 205°F over time. Low and slow, or pressure, gets there; rushing on high heat squeezes the meat dry before it gets tender.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Tough, won't shred | Not done. Keep cooking until a fork twists with no resistance (about 205°F) |
| Dry and stringy | Overcooked or not tossed with enough liquid. Toss with more reduced braising liquid |
| Tastes medicinal | Too much clove. Start at ¼ tsp; add more lime and a pinch of salt to rebalance |
| Too spicy | Fewer chipotles next time; serve with crema or slaw to cool it now |
| Flat or muddy | Needs acid and salt. Fresh lime and a pinch of salt at the end |
| Burn warning in pressure cooker | Brown bits stuck to the pot. Deglaze with broth and scrape before sealing |

# Chick-fil-A Sauce (Copycat)
### Mayo, honey, two mustards and smoky BBQ sauce. Five minutes, keeps a week

**Makes:** about ¾ cup | **Active:** 5 min | **Total:** 5 min, plus 1 hr rest for best flavor
**Equipment:** Small bowl, whisk

## Ingredients

- 2 Tbsp honey (warm it a few seconds so it mixes in)
- 2 Tbsp yellow mustard
- 1 Tbsp Dijon mustard
- 2 Tbsp smoky BBQ sauce (a hickory-style bottled sauce or the book's Spicy House Red Glaze)
- 1 tsp fresh lemon juice
- ½ cup mayonnaise

## Steps

1. **Whisk the honey and mustards together first.** Honey blends into mustard easily but clumps if it hits cold mayo.
2. **Add the BBQ sauce and lemon juice** and whisk.
3. **Whisk in the mayo** until smooth and evenly orange.
4. **Rest 1 hour in the fridge.** The flavors blend and it thickens slightly. Keeps up to a week, covered.
5. **Taste and adjust:** sweeter = more honey, tangier = more yellow mustard or lemon, smokier = more BBQ sauce.

### Variations
- **Spicy:** add 1–2 tsp hot sauce or a pinch of cayenne.
- **Lighter:** swap half the mayo for plain Greek yogurt.
- **Polynesian-style twist:** swap the BBQ sauce for sweet chili sauce.

## Use It On

Chicken tenders and sandwiches, crispy-skin chicken thighs, BBQ wings, smash burgers, fries, baked sweet potatoes, and as a wrap or slider spread.

## Why This Works (Five Pillars)

**Salt.** Mustard, BBQ sauce and mayo all carry salt, so none is added. Taste before you add any.

**Fat.** Mayo is the creamy base that carries everything and coats food so the sauce clings.

**Acid.** Two mustards and lemon bring the tang that keeps it from tasting like sweet mayo. Yellow mustard gives the classic flavor; Dijon adds a sharper bite.

**Sweet.** Honey is the main sweetness; BBQ sauce adds a second, deeper sweetness from molasses or brown sugar.

**Umami.** BBQ sauce brings tomato and smoke. That's what separates this from plain honey mustard.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Honey clumps | Honey was cold or added to mayo first. Warm it and mix it with the mustard first |
| Too sweet | More yellow mustard or a squeeze of lemon |
| Tastes like plain honey mustard | Needs more BBQ sauce, ideally a smoky one |
| Too thin | Rest it in the fridge, or add a spoonful more mayo |
